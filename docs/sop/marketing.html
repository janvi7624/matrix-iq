02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This SOP explains how marketing work is requested, assigned, produced, technically reviewed and delivered inside MatrixIQ, and how the shared materials library is curated and selectively published to channel partners.

It is written for three audiences at once: the colleague who needs a brochure, the marketing team member who makes it, and the technical reviewer who checks that the specifications on it are right. Because requests also arrive from external channel partners, it explains what partners see of this process and what they do not.

2.4 Intended audience

  • Primary — The Marketing team: members who produce the work and managers who approve, assign and commit timelines.
  • Primary — Anyone who raises a marketing request — Sales, Technical, Operations, Administration.
  • Secondary — Technical team members who perform technical review on marketing material.
  • Reference — Partner-facing colleagues who need to know what collateral is published externally.

2.5 Scope and limitations

In scope. The Marketing Requests workflow end to end, the Materials library, and the partner publication rule.

Out of scope. Brand guidelines, creative standards, external agency management, and the Partner Portal's own screens — those are in the Partner Portal SOP.

  • MatrixIQ stores a link to a document in most cases, not the file itself. Access in the source system is a separate matter — see §6.6.
  • Approval authority resolves from the Marketing department's managers in Department Master. A change there changes who may approve.
  • Details the application could not confirm are marked To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.
Important: the workflow is richer than the summary

The MatrixIQ Feature Reference summarises the workflow as submitted → assigned → accepted → in progress → technical review → delivered. That is a fair reading of the shape, but it is not the vocabulary the application uses, and it omits the separate approval and timeline steps. §7 and §8 document the statuses the application actually records. Where this SOP and the reference differ, the application has been treated as the source of truth.

03Introduction

3.1 What the Marketing module does

Marketing Requests turns "can you make me a brochure" into a tracked piece of work with an owner, a committed date and a reviewed result. Materials keeps everything that has already been made in one searchable place — and decides which of it channel partners may see.

A request begins as a structured brief: what is needed, for which product line, by when, with reference files attached. A Marketing manager reviews it, commits a delivery timeline, and assigns it to a team member who accepts or declines. The member prepares the work and — where the content makes technical claims — sends it to a named Technical team member for review. Once the technical decision is recorded, the final files are delivered to the original requester. A comment thread runs alongside throughout.

3.2 Why it exists

  • A brief that is actually complete. The four-step wizard asks for the product line, the collateral type, the description, the priority and the deadline before anything is submitted.
  • A timeline that means something. A delivery date is committed once by a manager and cannot afterwards be moved, so it is a commitment rather than an estimate.
  • Technical claims get checked. Specifications on a datasheet are reviewed by the Technical team before the work is delivered.
  • Collateral stops being lost. The Materials library organises documents in folders, by product and by category, and makes them searchable.
  • Partner publication is explicit. Nothing reaches a channel partner unless someone deliberately marked it — and marked every folder above it.

3.3 What users can and cannot do

4.3 Who counts as a "Marketing manager"

Resolved from the organisation, not from a role

A Marketing manager is whoever is listed as a manager of the Marketing department in Department Master. Accounts that see every department — Super Admin and Admin — also qualify. If no Marketing department is configured or it has no manager, MatrixIQ falls back to allowing any privileged account, so a request can never get permanently stuck.

Holding the marketing role does not by itself make someone a Marketing manager. The role identifies the team; the department manager list identifies the approver.

4.4 Who may curate the Materials library

Deliberately wider than approval authority

Adding, renaming, re-describing and removing documents and folders is open to anyone in the Marketing department, plus everyone who qualifies as a Marketing manager. This is a deliberate separation: publishing collateral is ordinary team work, whereas approving other departments' requests, their timelines and their assignment is not. The two should not require the same grant.

Everyone else with access to the module can read and search the library but not change it.

4.5 The partner boundary

InternalPartner
Sees the full internal status vocabulary, the assigned member, the technical reviewer, internal remarks and prepared content.Sees a deliberately coarser four-step position, their own submitted details, and the finished files once delivered. Nothing else.
Sees every document in the Materials library.Sees only documents explicitly published, inside a folder chain that is also published.
Can assign, approve, review and deliver.Can raise a request, comment on their own, and download delivered files.
Partner-facing information is built from an allow-list

What a partner sees of a marketing request is assembled from a fixed list of permitted fields — never by removing sensitive ones from the internal record. That is why adding a new internal field never accidentally exposes it. The practical consequence for you: internal remarks, prepared content, technical instructions and the names of the assigned member and reviewer are never visible to a partner, so write them as internal notes.

06Detailed Step-by-Step Procedures

6.1 Raising a marketing request

Preconditions

  • You are signed in with any internal account.
  • You know what you need, for which product line, and by when.
  • Any reference files — existing artwork, a specification, a client's brand sheet — are to hand.

Procedure — the four-step wizard

  1. Open Marketing → Marketing Requests and start a new request.
  2. Step 1 — Request & Category.
    • Request Title * — for example Product Brochure for Smart City Tender.
    • Product Category * — AV, Robotics, AI Video Analytics, System Integration, VisitIQ VMS or Other.
    • Collateral Type — Brochure / Flyer, Social Media Post, Banner / Standee Design, Video / Reel, Email Campaign, Website Update, Product Photography, Event Collateral, or Other.
    • Description of Requirement * — the brief. Describe the audience, the key specifications, dimensions, where it will be used and any branding notes.
    • Related Sales Project (Optional) — link the deal this is for.
  3. Step 2 — Priority & Deadline.
    PriorityWhat it signals
    UrgentNeeded within a day or two.
    HighNeeded this week.
    MediumNeeded in the next couple of weeks.
    LowNo rush — whenever it fits the queue.
    Also set the Required Deadline / Needed By Date (Optional).
  4. Step 3 — Files & Details. Attach reference files, and add Additional Information (Optional) — client requirements, technical specifications, reference links or notes.
  5. Step 4 — Review & Submit. Check the summary: product category, title, type, priority, required deadline, attachments, description and additional information. Submit.
  6. A confirmation appears — Request sent to Marketing! — with the category and requester. From there you can Submit Another Request or View All My Requests.
Write the brief properly the first time

The quality of the description decides how many rounds the work takes. State the audience, the output size or format, where it will appear, and anything that must not change. Attaching an existing piece you want it to resemble saves more time than any amount of later comment.

6.2 Reviewing, approving and declining

A new request arrives at Submitted / Awaiting Marketing. Requests from channel partners are badged as such in the queue.

6.2.1 Approving

  1. Open the request and read the brief and attachments.
  2. Select the approve action. Only a Marketing manager may do this, and only while the request is still awaiting review.
  3. The status becomes Approved.

6.2.2 Committing the delivery timeline

  1. On an Approved request, set the expected delivery date, with remarks if useful.
  2. Save. The status becomes Timeline Set.
The timeline can be committed only once

A delivery timeline is a real commitment, not a moving target. Once set it cannot be changed: MatrixIQ refuses with "A delivery timeline has already been committed for this request and cannot be changed." Agree the date before you set it.

It also has a second effect: once a timeline exists the requester can no longer cancel the request.

Order matters — approve and set the timeline before assigning

Setting a timeline requires the request to be at Approved. Assigning a request that is still at Submitted moves it straight to Marketing In Progress — past the point at which a timeline can be set. If a committed date is wanted, follow this order: approve → set timeline → assign.

6.2.3 Declining

  1. Select the decline action on a request still awaiting review.
  2. Enter a reason. It is required, and the requester reads it.
  3. The status becomes Declined.

A request that has moved past the awaiting-review stage cannot be declined: "Only a request still awaiting review can be declined." Use the comment thread and a conversation with the requester instead.

6.3 Assignment, acceptance and decline

6.3.1 Assigning to a marketing member

  1. Open the request and choose a marketing member to assign it to.
  2. Save. The assignment is recorded as awaiting their confirmation, and if the request was still at Submitted its status becomes Marketing In Progress.
  3. Set or change the priority here if the queue has shifted.

Only a marketing reviewer or manager may assign. A Technical team member may also be nominated at this point as the eventual reviewer.

6.3.2 Accepting an assignment

  1. The assigned member opens the request.
  2. They select accept. The assignment is confirmed; the request's status does not change.
Acceptance is a gate, not a formality

Until the assigned member accepts, they cannot send work to technical review, cannot record a final submission, and cannot move the request through its working statuses. MatrixIQ refuses with "You must accept this assignment before you can act on it." A manager or reviewer is never blocked by this.

6.3.3 Declining an assignment

  1. The assigned member selects decline.
  2. A reason is required: "A reason is required to decline an assignment."
  3. The request returns to Submitted / Awaiting Marketing and the assignment is marked declined, so a manager can reassign it.

Only the assigned member may accept or decline, and only while the assignment is awaiting their confirmation.

6.4 Doing the work and technical review

6.4.1 Working the request

The assigned member moves the request through working statuses as the job progresses. The actions available are:

Waiting for Information is the one status the requester must act on

It is the only status that tells the requester the ball is in their court — and the only one surfaced to a channel partner as needing their action. Use it, with a clear remark, rather than letting a request sit in progress while you wait for an answer.

6.4.2 Sending work to technical review

  1. Open the request. You must be the assigned member (and have accepted), or a marketing reviewer or manager.
  2. Record the prepared content, attach the marketing files, and add any marketing remarks.
  3. Add technical instructions — what specifically the reviewer should check.
  4. Select the Technical Team member who will review. One must be nominated; MatrixIQ refuses otherwise.
  5. Send. The status becomes Pending Technical Review.

If you are not the assigned member, MatrixIQ names who is: "This request is assigned to [name]. Only the assigned marketing member can prepare content and send to Technical."

6.4.3 Recording the technical decision

  1. The nominated technical reviewer opens the request and reads the prepared content, the attached files and the technical instructions.
  2. They record one of two decisions:
    DecisionRemarksResulting status
    ApproveOptionalTechnical Approved
    Request changesRequiredChanges Requested — "Please provide remarks detailing the requested technical changes."
  3. On a change request, the marketing member uses Work on changes to return the request to Marketing In Progress, makes the corrections, and sends it to technical review again.
What a technical review is for

It checks that the claims on the collateral are correct — model numbers, specifications, capabilities, compliance statements. It is not a design review. Write technical instructions that ask a technical question.

6.5 Delivery, cancellation and the comment thread

6.5.1 Delivering the final files

  1. Once the content is right, record the final submission: the finished files and any notes for the requester.
  2. Submit. The status becomes Completed, and the requester is notified.
  3. File the finished collateral in the Materials library (§6.6) so it can be found again.
The requester sees nothing until you deliver

Final submission notes and files are a working draft right up to the moment of delivery. For a channel partner they are withheld entirely until the request is Completed, precisely so an in-flight draft is never mistaken for the deliverable. Do not treat the final-submission field as a place to share work in progress — use the comment thread.

6.5.2 Cancelling a request

  1. The person who raised the request — or a privileged account — opens it.
  2. Select cancel. The status becomes Cancelled.
Cancellation closes early

A request can only be withdrawn while it is still awaiting review. Once a reviewer has committed to it, MatrixIQ refuses: "This request already has a committed timeline and can no longer be cancelled." After that point, talk to Marketing and let them decline or close it — so that the commitment someone made is not silently undone.

6.5.3 Cancelled, Declined and Changes Requested — three different things

OutcomeWho ends itMeaning
CancelledThe requesterWe no longer need this. Only possible before a timeline is committed.
DeclinedA marketing reviewerMarketing will not take this on, with a written reason the requester reads. Only possible before a timeline is committed.
Changes RequestedThe technical reviewerNot an ending — the work goes back to Marketing with remarks and continues.

6.5.4 The comment thread

Every request carries a comment thread. Use it for anything that is a conversation rather than a status change — clarifying a brief, sharing a draft for an opinion, agreeing a date before it is committed. Comments are visible to everyone who can see the request, which for a partner request includes the partner.

6.6 Materials — adding and organising

6.6.1 Adding a document

  1. Open Marketing → Materials.
  2. Start a new document and complete:
    • Product / Document name * — for example Allbotix X200. Everything filed under the same name appears as one entry, which is how several documents for one product are grouped.
    • Category * — see the table below.
    • Where is this document? * — the destination folder.
    • Document name and Description — what it is for, and who it should go to.
  3. Choose the source. There are three:
    SourceWhat you provide, and what to check
    Drive linkA Google Drive file or folder address. MatrixIQ stores the link, not the file. Anyone opening it needs access in Drive — if it is set to Restricted, share it with the team first.
    Upload a fileThe file itself is uploaded to MatrixIQ. Use this where Drive access cannot be relied on.
    Web linkAny web address, including a video. MatrixIQ stores the link, not the page. Open it in a private window before adding it — if it needs a sign-in the team does not have, the link will lead nowhere.
  4. Save.
Reference clarification

The Feature Reference describes Materials as "add a document by Drive link". The application supports three sources — Drive link, uploaded file, and web link. Choose deliberately: a link is only as reliable as the permissions on the thing it points at.

6.6.2 Categories

CategoryWhat belongs in it
DatasheetTechnical specifications, one per product.
Case StudyInstallations and success stories, by client or site.
Company ProfileThe corporate profile and overview decks.
BrochureProduct brochures, catalogues and leaflets.
PresentationDecks.

Datasheet and Case Study lead the list because they are what people come to the library for. Each category carries a hint on screen describing what belongs in it, so that two people filing the same document choose the same place.

6.6.3 Folders

  1. Create a folder with a Folder name * — for example Robots — and a Description saying what belongs in it.
  2. Folders nest, so a tree can be built: product line, then sub-line, then document type.
  3. Move documents into the right folder as the library grows.

6.6.4 Finding something

Use the search box — it matches by product, document and description — or browse the Folders tree. Documents are grouped by product name and tabbed by category.

6.7 Materials — sharing with partners

Read this section fully before publishing anything

Publishing a document makes it available to external companies. Internal sales decks, pricing, competitor comparisons and anything commercially sensitive must never be marked shared.

6.7.1 The two-gate rule

Both gates must be open

A document is visible to a partner only if both are true:

  1. The document itself is marked Share with partners; and
  2. Every folder on its path to the top is also shared.

The second condition is the one that is easy to get wrong. It is not enough to share the immediate parent: a shared Datasheets folder sitting inside an unshared Internal folder stays hidden — which is exactly the behaviour you want, because the boundary Marketing drew must be the boundary that holds. A document at the top level, in no folder at all, is governed by its own setting alone.

If the folder structure is ever broken or circular, publication fails closed: nothing is published. A library whose structure is corrupt must not default to publishing.

6.7.2 Publishing a document

  1. Open the document for editing.
  2. Tick Share with partners. The on-screen note confirms: "Publishes this document to the Partner Portal's Marketing Materials library."
  3. If the folder is not yet shared, MatrixIQ warns you immediately: "This folder is not shared with partners yet, so the document still won't appear for them. Share the folder too — and every folder above it." Act on that warning; ticking the document alone achieves nothing.
  4. Save.
  5. Verify. Ask a partner contact, or someone who can see the partner library, to confirm the document is visible — and, just as importantly, that nothing else has become visible alongside it.

6.7.3 Publishing a folder

  1. Open the folder for editing.
  2. Tick Share this folder with partners and save.
  3. Repeat for every folder above it, up to the top.
Sharing a folder opens the path, not the contents

Marking a folder shared satisfies the second gate for the documents inside it. Each document still needs its own Share with partners tick. So sharing a folder does not publish everything in it — but it does mean that anything in it which is already individually ticked becomes visible at that moment. Before sharing a folder, check what inside it is already marked.

6.7.4 Unpublishing

Untick Share with partners on the document, or unshare any folder on its path. Either closes a gate and removes it from the partner library.

Unpublishing does not recall what has been downloaded

A partner who has already opened or downloaded a document still has it. Unpublishing stops future access; it is not a retraction. If something was published in error, unpublish immediately and tell whoever owns the partner relationship.

6.7.5 Narrowing publication to specific partners

Not available from this screen

MatrixIQ can restrict a published document to particular partner companies rather than all of them. No control for this was found on the Materials screen — the on-screen tick is a simple on or off, and in practice a published document is visible to every partner. Whether per-partner publication can be configured by another route is To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.

Plan on the assumption that publishing means "every partner". Do not publish a document intended for one partner only.

Why the partner view is coarser

The internal vocabulary names who is holding the ticket — Pending Technical Review, Changes Requested, Marketing Final Review. That tells a partner about our internal handoffs and, worse, reads as a problem on their side when it is entirely routine. In review says the true thing without the organisation chart.

8.5 When a request is flagged overdue

A request is overdue when the current date has passed its committed delivery date, or — where no timeline was committed — its needed-by date. Requests that are Completed, Declined or Cancelled are never flagged. The flag is recomputed each time the queue is read, so it is always current.

10.2 How e-mail behaves

E-mail can fail silently

Mail is sent through Amazon SES using server settings. If those are not provisioned, MatrixIQ skips the send and continues without an error. The request queue is the reliable work list — open it, do not wait to be told.

12.2 Recommended practice

Accurate briefing and data entry

  • Write the brief as if the person reading it has never seen the product. State audience, format, size and where it will appear.
  • Choose the most specific collateral type. Other makes the queue harder to plan.
  • Link the sales project where one exists — it is how collateral gets traced back to the deal that needed it.
  • Set a priority that is honest. If everything is Urgent, nothing is.

Attachments and documents

  • Attach references at submission, not later in the comment thread.
  • Prefer an uploaded file over a link when the document must remain readable regardless of anyone's Drive permissions.
  • Open every web link in a private window before adding it to the library.
  • File delivered collateral into Materials as the last step of every request.

Timely decisions

  • Accept or decline an assignment the same day. Declining promptly with a reason is better than silence.
  • Review the queue for overdue requests at a fixed point each week.
  • Commit timelines you can keep. The date cannot be moved afterwards.
  • Record technical decisions quickly — the work is stopped while they are pending.

Confidentiality and partner publication

  • Before publishing, ask: would it be acceptable for every partner to have this? Assume publication means "all partners".
  • Never publish pricing, internal sales decks, competitor comparisons or anything naming a client without that client's agreement.
  • Review the published library periodically against what is still current. Review cadence: to be confirmed.
  • Write internal remarks as internal notes — but never write anything you would be uncomfortable with a colleague reading, since the comment thread is visible to everyone on the request, partners included on partner requests.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Search Materials before raising a request. The thing may already exist.
  • File documents for one product under one consistent product name — that is what groups them as a single entry.
  • Do not raise a second request because the first is slow. Comment on the existing one.

Keeping an auditable history

  • Use the comment thread rather than e-mail or chat. It is the only record attached to the request.
  • Write decline and change-request reasons that will still make sense in six months.
  • Record the final submission notes properly — they are what the requester reads alongside the files.

14Glossary and Appendix

14.1 Glossary

Allow-list
A fixed list of fields a partner is permitted to see. Partner-facing information is built from one, never by removing sensitive fields from the internal record.
Assigned member
The marketing team member a request has been given to. They must accept before they can act.
Collateral type
What kind of marketing output is wanted — brochure, social post, banner, video and so on.
Comment thread
The conversation attached to a request, visible to everyone who can see it.
Committed timeline
The expected delivery date set once by a Marketing manager. It cannot afterwards be changed, and its existence blocks cancellation.
Folder chain
The path from a document up to the top of the library. Every folder on it must be shared for the document to reach partners.
Fails closed
Where the folder structure cannot be resolved, nothing is published. The safe direction.
Marketing manager
Whoever is listed as a manager of the Marketing department in Department Master, plus accounts that see every department.
Needed-by date
The requester's wish. Distinct from the committed delivery timeline, which is Marketing's commitment.
Partner request
A marketing request raised from the Partner Portal. It enters the same queue, badged as such, and follows the same workflow.
Prepared content
The internal working field in which the assigned member records the draft before technical review. Never visible to a partner.
Technical instructions
What the assigned member asks the technical reviewer to check. Internal only.
Technical reviewer
The Technical team member nominated to check the technical accuracy of the content.
Two-gate rule
A document reaches partners only if the document is shared and every folder above it is shared.
