02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This SOP explains how MatrixIQ is configured: who has an account, what each role may do, how departments and their managers are defined, which modules exist and who sees them, what the product catalogue contains, what appears on a quotation, how external lead sources connect, and how the daily reporting regime is set.

These screens do not process business transactions. They decide the rules every other screen obeys. A single change here can widen or remove access for dozens of people, alter the price on a customer-facing document, or silence a reminder the whole company depends on. This document is therefore written as much to prevent unsafe changes as to enable necessary ones.

In scope. The eleven Administration submodules listed on the cover, the effect each has on the rest of MatrixIQ, and the safe way to change each one.

Out of scope. Server deployment, database administration, environment variables, backups, and the Technical Project Management module's own separate Tab Access permission matrix, which is administered by the Technical Manager rather than from this section.

  • This document describes configuration as it stood on 27 October 2026. Because these very screens change configuration, the document can be made inaccurate by its own readers.
  • Details the application could not confirm are marked To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.
Standing warning — applies to this entire document

Changes to role permissions, module availability, department managers, tax settings, product pricing and document numbering take effect for every affected user, in most cases within about half a minute and without any announcement. Several have no undo.

Do not change production configuration without appropriate authorisation and internal approval. Record what you changed, when, and on whose instruction — the Audit Log does not capture every configuration edit (see §8.5).

03Introduction

3.1 What the Administration module does

Administration is where MatrixIQ is told how to behave. Everything here changes the platform without a software release.

A new employee gets an account and a role. A role is given a set of permissions. A department is created and given managers — and because approval routing across the whole platform is resolved from departments and their managers, that one act decides who approves travel, who signs off reimbursements and whose daily reports are visible to whom. Modules are switched on or off and shown to particular roles. Products and prices feed every quotation. Company details, tax and terms feed every quotation PDF. The daily reporting regime — working days, cut-off time, reminders and warnings — is set here too.

3.2 Why it exists

  • The organisation changes faster than software. People join, teams are renamed, a department gains a manager. None of that should need a developer.
  • One definition, used everywhere. Department managers defined once drive approvals, visibility and routing across every module, so the rules cannot drift apart.
  • Visibility and capability stay aligned. Disabling a module hides the tile and closes the data behind it, so a hidden screen is genuinely unreachable.
  • New business needs without new code. The Custom Module Builder creates a working module — fields, records, approval and export — from a form.

3.3 What administrators can and cannot do

3.4 The four things that decide who sees what

MatrixIQ resolves visibility from four inputs working together. Understanding this is the single most useful thing in this document.

1. The roleRole ManagementThe role key on the account — Sales, HR, Accounts, Marketing, Back Office, a Technical role, or an admin tier.
2. The privileged flagRole ManagementA flag on the role granting broad, organisation-wide reach. Super Admin, Admin and Manager carry it.
3. The departmentDepartment MasterWhich department the user belongs to, and which departments they manage. Managers drive approval routing everywhere.
4. Module visibilityModule ManagerWhether a module is enabled at all, and which roles may see it. Disabling closes the data as well as the tile.
Plus: named grantsconfigured per moduleA few sensitive modules are granted to named individuals rather than to a role — see §4.4.
Plus: the partner boundarypartner accountAn account linked to a partner company is confined to the Partner Portal and refused everywhere else, in both directions.
Privileged is not a master key

The privileged flag is true for every manager account, which covers every department's managers — Sales, Accounts, Technical and the rest. Several areas therefore deliberately refuse it and name their roles explicitly instead: the Technical Project Management modules, the HR modules, the Accounts payment queue, the Audit Log, the Daily Report Configuration page, and the whole Partner Portal. Granting someone the Manager role does not give them these.

4.2 Deletion is Super Admin only

The single clearest line in MatrixIQ

Any delete action anywhere under the Administration interfaces is refused for every role except Super Admin, with the message "Forbidden — superadmin only". An Admin can create and edit everything they can see; they cannot delete. Plan removals accordingly.

4.3 Super Admin accounts are invisible to other roles

Super Admin accounts do not appear to anyone except another Super Admin. They are absent from the Employee Directory, from profile views, and from every assignment, handover and manager picker. This is deliberate: the organisation's ultimate account holders should not be enumerable from an ordinary administrator session, not merely protected from editing.

4.4 Modules granted to named individuals, not roles

Three modules are granted per person on top of the Super Admin and Admin tier. The hr role on its own grants none of them. This cannot be changed from Role Management or Module Manager.

06Detailed Step-by-Step Procedures

Each procedure states what must be true before you start, what to do, what happens afterwards, and how to recover. Read the risk note at the head of each subsection before acting.

6.1 User Management

Risk level — high

Creating or changing an account grants or removes a person's access to company data. Deactivating an account can strand records that person owns.

6.1.1 Creating a single employee account

Preconditions

  • You hold Super Admin, Admin or Manager.
  • The department already exists in Department Master, and the role already exists in Role Management.
  • You have the person's correct e-mail address — the welcome message goes there.

Procedure

  1. Open Administration → User Management.
  2. Select New employee account. The form opens.
  3. Complete the fields:
    • Full name — as it should appear throughout MatrixIQ.
    • Employee ID (optional).
    • Username — the login name. It must be unique and is the identity every permission and audit entry is keyed to.
    • Password — minimum six characters. The user is required to change it on first sign-in.
    • Email, Phone.
    • Department — chosen from Department Master.
    • Designation — free text, for example Sales Executive.
    • Role — chosen from Role Management.
  4. Select Add employee, or Cancel to abandon.

What happens next

  • The account is created and appears in the directory as Active.
  • A welcome e-mail is sent to the address supplied, provided the platform's e-mail service is configured. If it is not, the send is skipped silently — see §10.2.
  • The user is held at the password-change screen until they set their own password.
Pick the username carefully

Several grants in MatrixIQ are keyed to the username, not the display name — the named-individual module grants in §4.4 among them. Changing a display name is harmless; a username is the account's identity and should be treated as permanent.

6.1.2 Bulk importing users

Bulk import is a two-phase operation: preview first, commit second. Nothing is created until you commit.

  1. From User Management, select Import from Excel.
  2. Upload the employee workbook. MatrixIQ expects the columns of the standard employee detail sheet — employee ID, name, e-mail and the rest — and tolerates minor variations in how the headings are spelled.
  3. Read the preview table before doing anything else. Each row is classified:
    Row statusMeaning
    createdWill be created as a new account on commit.
    existingAn account already matches. It will not be duplicated.
    needsReviewSomething is ambiguous or incomplete. Decide before committing.
    errorThe row cannot be processed as it stands.
    skippedDeliberately not processed.
    The preview columns are Row, Name, Employee ID, Department, Username, Status and Notes.
  4. Correct the spreadsheet and re-upload if rows need fixing. Start Over discards the preview.
  5. Commit the import. MatrixIQ creates the accounts and shows a result table: Employee, Employee ID, Username, Temporary Password, Role, Status.
  6. Capture the temporary passwords before leaving the screen. Distribute them to each person through a secure channel.
The temporary password list is shown once

Whether the result table can be regenerated after you navigate away is To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application. Treat it as one-time. If a password is lost, use Reset password on the individual account instead of re-running the import.

6.1.3 Editing, resetting and deactivating an account

  1. Find the person in the directory. Each row carries an actions menu.
  2. Edit — change name, phone, e-mail, employee ID, designation or role.
  3. Reset password — issues a new password; the user must change it at next sign-in.
  4. Resend welcome email — use when the original never arrived. MatrixIQ asks for confirmation first.
  5. Deactivate — MatrixIQ asks "Deactivate this employee?" before proceeding. Activate reverses it.
  6. Select several rows to activate or deactivate them together.
Deactivate is not the same as handling a leaver

Deactivating stops the person signing in. It does not reassign the projects, tasks, leads and quotations they own, and it does not remove them from any department's manager list — although MatrixIQ does stop treating a deactivated account as an active manager when it resolves routing. For a genuine departure, use Employee Exit (§6.1.5) before deactivating.

6.1.4 Viewing a user's activity history

  1. Open the actions menu on the person's row and select View activity. Select Hide activity to collapse it again.
  2. Read the history to answer questions such as when the account last signed in and what it has been doing. For a full status-change trail, use the Audit Log (§6.11) instead.

6.1.5 Handling a departing employee (Employee Exit)

Employee Exit is at /employee-exit, in the Administration section. It is open to anyone who manages at least one department or who sees organisation-wide — a department manager can exit their own team's people.

  1. Open Employee Exit and choose the departing employee.
  2. MatrixIQ produces a summary of currently open work owned by that person. Only open work is listed; closed and historical records deliberately keep their original owner:
    Record typeWhat counts as "still open"
    ProjectsNot yet won and not yet lost.
    TasksNot completed and not cancelled.
    LeadsNot yet converted into a project.
    QuotationsStill at draft or sent — not approved or rejected.
  3. Choose a new owner for each item, or use Reassign everything to… followed by Apply to all. The departing employee can never be chosen as their own replacement, and only active employees are offered.
  4. Commit. Every reassignment is written in one operation, so a half-finished exit cannot exist.
  5. MatrixIQ writes one audit line per record and notifies each new owner, then marks the employee inactive.
  6. Select Do another to process a second leaver, or Close.
Finish the exit checklist outside MatrixIQ too

Employee Exit is a work-reassignment tool. Remove the person from any department's manager list in Department Master, and from any named-individual grant (§4.4), as separate steps.

Do not plan access around the retired role.

6.2.2 Creating or cloning a role

  1. To start from nothing, enter a Role name and an optional Description, then select Add role. A new role starts with no permissions and is not privileged.
  2. To start from an existing role, select Clone on its row. Cloning copies the description, the privileged flag and the whole permission matrix. This is the recommended way to create a role — start from the closest existing one and remove what is not needed.
  3. Open the new role and set its permissions (§6.2.3).
  4. Assign the role to a test account before assigning it widely, and sign in as that account to confirm what it can actually reach.

6.2.3 Setting permissions

  1. Select Edit on the role.
  2. Set the five Global capabilities where appropriate:
    CapabilityEffect
    Manage settingsChange Application Settings.
    Manage usersCreate and edit accounts.
    Manage rolesChange this very matrix.
    Manage departmentsChange Department Master.
    View all departmentsOrganisation-wide data visibility. The widest grant in MatrixIQ.
  3. Work through the per-module matrix. Each module offers ten actions: view, create, edit, delete, export, print, approve, reject, assign, manage. Expand All and Collapse All help navigate; Select All Permissions and Clear All Permissions act on everything at once.
  4. Set the Privileged flag only when the role genuinely needs organisation-wide reach. Read the warning below first.
  5. Select Save.
How an unset permission behaves

A permission left unset is not simply "no". For the newer data-driven modules, an explicit tick or cross always wins; where nothing is configured, MatrixIQ falls back to the role's privileged flag. That is why a brand-new non-privileged role starts with nothing, and why ticking the privileged flag can grant far more than the matrix appears to show.

Use Select All Permissions with great care

It ticks every action on every module at once, including delete. Combined with the privileged flag it produces a role close to Super Admin. Use it as a starting point for narrowing, never as a final state.

6.2.4 Deleting or disabling a role

  1. Reassign every account holding the role first. Check User Management before you act.
  2. Set the role's Status to inactive rather than deleting it where possible. An inactive role grants nothing — every permission check requires the role to be active.
  3. If deletion is genuinely required, a Super Admin must perform it, and the role must not be built-in.

6.3 Department Master

Risk level — very high, and widely under-appreciated

Department managers are the routing table for approvals across the whole platform. Changing them re-routes live work.

6.3.1 What departments actually control

AreaWhat is resolved from Department Master
ReimbursementWhich manager approves an employee's monthly sheet; the HR department's managers make the HR decision.
Travel ScheduleThe manager stage (the employee's own department), the HR stages (the HR department's managers), and the Administration stage (the Administration department's managers).
Accounts payment queueManagers of the Accounts department gain access regardless of role.
Back OfficeManagers of the Back Office department can act on Delivery Challans regardless of role.
BOM RequestManagers of Administration hold the administration stage; managers of Accounts hold the payment stage.
Marketing RequestsManagers of Marketing approve, assign and set delivery timelines. Everyone in the Marketing department can curate the materials library.
LeadsManagers of Sales or GEM - Sales may route captured leads to representatives.
Daily reportsA manager sees and may act on the departments they manage. Team Tasks and Team Daily Reports are gated on being a manager of some department, whatever the role.
Technical Project ManagementOnly the four technical departments see TMS modules at all.
Department names matter

Several routing rules look for a department by name. The Administration stage of a BOM request looks for Administration; the Accounts grants look for Accounts; the Back Office grant looks for Back Office. HR is matched more tolerantly, on any department whose name begins with "HR" — which is why "HR & Admin" still works.

Renaming a department can silently break an approval route. Where no manager is found, most stages fall back to allowing any privileged user, so work does not get permanently stuck — but it reaches the wrong people. Treat a rename as a change requiring approval and a check of every stage listed above.

6.3.2 Creating, renaming and ordering departments

  1. Open Administration → Department Master. The table shows Order, Name, Description, Manager and Status.
  2. To add one: enter a Department name and an optional Description, then select Add department. New departments are created active and placed at the end of the order.
  3. To rename or re-describe: select Edit, change the values, then Save. Check the routing table in §6.3.1 before renaming.
  4. To reorder: use the reorder control. The order governs how departments are presented in pickers and lists; it carries no permission meaning.
  5. Use the search box to find a department in a long list.
  6. Deletion is Super Admin only. Prefer setting the status to inactive — only active departments are offered in pickers and used in routing.

6.3.3 Appointing department managers

  1. Select Edit on the department.
  2. Choose one or more managers. A department may have several. If the list shows "No users available", no eligible active accounts exist yet.
  3. Select Save. The change takes effect across the platform within about half a minute.
  4. Tell the people concerned. Being appointed a manager silently adds approval duties and widens what they see.
A manager's role does not have to be "Manager"

Any account can be a department's manager — a Technical Manager, a Sales user, anyone. Management is a configured relationship, not a role. This is why screens such as Team Tasks and Team Daily Reports are gated on "manages some department" rather than on a role list.

Equally, holding the manager role without being listed here gives organisation-wide privileged reach in some places but no approval authority in the chains above.

Deactivated managers are not removed automatically

Deactivating an account does not take it off any department's manager list. MatrixIQ stops treating inactive accounts as managers when it resolves routing, so approvals do not go to a departed person — but the stale entry remains on screen and the department may be left with no effective manager. Remove departed managers explicitly.

6.4 Module Manager

Risk level — very high

Disabling a module removes it for everyone, administrators included, and closes the data behind it at the same moment.

6.4.1 Reading the screen

Modules are grouped by sidebar section — Sales, TMS, Workspace, HR, Marketing, Operations, Support, Accounts, Reports, Administration and the Partner Portal sections. Each row carries:

ColumnMeaning
OrderUp and down controls that move the module within its own section.
IconA dropdown of available icons. A module still carrying an older custom icon shows it with the note (legacy).
LabelThe name shown in the sidebar and on the dashboard. Editable. Modules created from the Custom Module Builder also show a Custom badge.
HrefThe address the tile opens. Read-only — it cannot be changed.
EnabledA tick box. Unticking it closes the module completely.
One column per roleA tick box per role, controlling who sees the tile.

6.4.2 Making a change

  1. Open Administration → Module Manager.
  2. Make your edits. Changed rows are highlighted, and sections whose order has changed are flagged.
  3. Select Save. Reordering within a section is saved per section.
  4. Confirm the result by signing in as an account holding an affected role, or by asking a colleague who holds one.

6.4.3 What disabling actually does

Disabling closes the data, not just the tile

A disabled module blocks everyone, including Super Admin, and its data interfaces close with it. A user who knows the address cannot reach the screen, and no other screen can read that module's data. This is deliberate: the navigation and the data behind it can never disagree.

Before disabling anything, confirm that no live workflow depends on it. Disabling Reimbursement, for example, stops employees submitting, stops managers approving, and removes the ability to read existing sheets.

6.4.4 What role visibility cannot do

Ticking a role column makes the tile visible to that role. It does not override the stricter, built-in rules on certain modules. Those rules are applied first and cannot be loosened from this screen:

If a tile is ticked but the screen still refuses

That is one of the rules above, not a fault. Module Manager can narrow access below these rules; it cannot widen beyond them.

6.5 Custom Module Builder

Risk level — medium

A custom module creates a real, user-facing module. Its fields are difficult to change once records exist.

6.5.1 Creating a custom module

  1. Open Administration → Custom Module Builder.
  2. Enter the Module name, for example Visitor Register.
  3. Choose an Icon and a Sidebar section — which group the tile appears under.
  4. Optionally choose an Approver role. Setting one makes every new record require approval; leaving it blank means records are active the moment they are saved.
  5. Define the Fields. For each one, give a Field label and a Type, and mark it required if it must be filled. Use Remove to drop a field.
    Available field types

Who sees a new custom module

A newly created custom module is made visible to Super Admin, Admin and Manager. To show it to other roles, open Module Manager and tick the relevant role columns on its row.

6.5.2 Capturing and approving records

  1. Select Open on the module, or use its sidebar tile.
  2. Add a record by completing the fields you defined. Attachments are supported.
  3. If the module has an approver role, the record is created as Pending approval. Otherwise it is created Active.
  4. A user holding the approver role sees pending records and can Approve or Reject them.

Who sees which records

Within a custom module, a user sees the records they created. A user holding the configured approver role additionally sees records still pending their approval. Broader visibility depends on the role's own permissions.

6.5.3 Exporting

From the module screen, Export CSV downloads the records currently listed, Print produces a printable view and Refresh reloads.

6.5.4 Changing a custom module later

Field changes affect existing records

Removing a field from a module that already holds records, or changing a field's type, risks making stored values unreadable. Export the records to CSV before any structural change, and prefer adding a new field to altering an existing one.

6.6 Products

Risk level — high

Prices here feed every new quotation, and bulk repricing can change hundreds of items at once.

6.6.1 Adding and editing a product

  1. Open Administration → Products.
  2. In Add product, complete: Product name, SKU / Product code, Category, Brand, Unit, Description, Default Qty, Base Price, Selling Price, Tax %, HSN/SAC (optional) and Discount %.
  3. Select + Add Product.
  4. To change one, find it with the search box or the All categories and All statuses filters, then select Edit, amend, and Save. Duplicate copies a product as the basis for a new one. Delete is Super Admin only.
  5. Set Status to Inactive to retire a product without deleting it. This is the preferred way to withdraw an item.

The list shows Name, SKU, Category, Brand, Unit, Base Price, Selling Price, Tax % and Status.

6.6.2 Bulk repricing

  1. Filter the list to exactly the products you intend to change, then select them.
  2. Choose the target field — Selling Price or Base Price.
  3. Choose the method — Adjust by % or Set to fixed amount, and enter the value.
  4. Check the selection count once more.
  5. Select Apply Bulk Update, or Clear selection to abandon.
Bulk repricing has no undo

Export the catalogue to CSV or XLSX before every bulk update. That export is the only straightforward way back. Apply the change to a small selection first and confirm the result before running it across the catalogue.

6.6.3 Import and export

  1. Use Export CSV or Export XLSX to download the catalogue. Do this before any import.
  2. To import, choose a .csv or .xlsx file. The expected columns are: SKU, Product Name, Category, Brand, Description, Unit, Default Qty, Base Price, Selling Price, Tax %, HSN/SAC, Discount %, Status.
  3. Rows are matched to existing products by SKU. A matching SKU updates that product; a new SKU creates one.
  4. Read the import summary that follows.
Import updates in place and has no preview

Unlike the user import, the product import does not offer a preview-then-commit step. A mistyped SKU column can rewrite the wrong products immediately. Always export first.

This screen is separate from Products. Products is the general quotation product master. Product Catalog sits on top of MatrixIQ's built-in engineering catalogues for AV, Robotics, AI Video Analytics and VisitIQ, letting you rename an item or change its prices without a software release.

6.7.1 How overrides work

An override is a layer, not a replacement

Each built-in catalogue has a base list of products. An override you save is applied on top of that base record. Where you change nothing, the base value continues to apply — and continues to benefit from updates to the base catalogue. An override on a product that has no base entry is a brand-new product you created, and carries the complete record rather than a partial change.

Two switches, in this order

Dry run computes and logs everything but sends nothing. Scheduler enabled arms the job. As configured, the scheduler is off and dry run is on. Always run in dry run first, read the delivery log, and only then turn dry run off.

  1. Set Dry run — compute and log, send nothing as required.

  3. Select Save Policy.

07Workflow and Approval Process

Administration has no approval chain of its own. A change is made and it takes effect. The governance therefore has to be procedural, and this section defines it.

7.1 The recommended change process

STEP 1Request
By: the person who needs the change · Record: what, why, and who is affected.
↓
STEP 2Assess the blast radius
By: the administrator · Ask: how many users, which workflows, is it reversible, does it reach a customer-facing document?
If it touches role permissions, module availability, department managers, tax, pricing or numbering — it requires written approval before proceeding.
↓
STEP 3Authorise
By: the change authority · Record: the approval, in writing, outside MatrixIQ. Change authority: to be confirmed.
↓
STEP 4Capture the current state
Action: export the product catalogue, note the current settings values, screenshot the role matrix or module row. This is the only rollback that exists for several of these screens.
↓
STEP 5Make the change
Scope: one change at a time, so that an unexpected effect can be attributed.
↓
STEP 6Verify
Action: confirm from an affected account, or produce a real document — not merely re-read the screen you just edited.
↓
STEP 7Communicate and record
Action: tell the affected users, and log the change in the organisation's own change register.

E-mail can fail silently

12.2 Recommended practice

Before any change

  • Capture the current state. For the product catalogue that means an export; for roles, modules and settings it means a screenshot or a transcription.
  • Obtain and record written authorisation for anything touching permissions, module availability, tax, pricing or numbering.
  • Make one change at a time, so an unexpected effect can be attributed.

Accounts and roles

  • Grant the narrowest role that does the job. Prefer cloning an existing role and removing rights to building one up from nothing.
  • Avoid creating privileged roles. The flag is broad, and several areas refuse it anyway — so it usually grants the wrong things while failing to grant the intended one.
  • Verify every new role from a test account before assigning it widely.
  • Review accounts and roles periodically against the staff list. Review cadence: to be confirmed.

Departments

  • Treat appointing or removing a department manager as a change to the approval chain, because that is what it is. Tell the person.
  • Never rename a department casually. Check every routing rule in §6.3.1 first.
  • Make sure every department that needs approvals has at least one active manager.

Products and pricing

  • Export before every bulk update and every import, without exception.
  • Test a bulk change on a small selection first.
  • Retire products by setting them inactive rather than deleting them — historical quotations remain readable.

Confidentiality and access control

  • Never share an administrator account. Every action is attributed to the signed-in username.
  • Distribute temporary passwords through a channel the recipient controls, never in a shared document.
  • Keep the number of Super Admin accounts to the minimum the organisation can operate with.
  • Treat the Audit Log as confidential: it records who did what, from which address.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Search before creating a department, a role, a product or an account.
  • Use the import preview to find accounts that already exist, rather than creating parallel ones.
  • Avoid two departments with similar names — routing rules match by name and will pick one.

Keeping an auditable history

  • Maintain a change register outside MatrixIQ. The Audit Log does not cover configuration.
  • Record the authorisation alongside the change.
  • Retain pre-change exports, dated, for as long as the organisation's retention policy requires.

14Glossary and Appendix

14.1 Glossary

Append-only
A record that can be added to but never edited or deleted. The Audit Log and the notification ledger are append-only.
Built-in role
A role shipped with MatrixIQ. It can be edited but never deleted.
Capability (global)
One of five platform-wide grants on a role: manage settings, manage users, manage roles, manage departments, view all departments.
Custom module
A module created from the Custom Module Builder, rendered by one generic engine rather than by code written for it.
Department manager
A person listed as a manager of a department in Department Master. This relationship, not a role, drives approval routing across the platform.
Href
The address a module tile opens. Shown read-only in Module Manager.
Module key
The stable internal identifier of a module, such as user-management. Permissions and visibility are keyed to it, so renaming a module's label is safe.
Named-individual grant
Access granted to particular usernames rather than to a role. Used for the three HR and Administration spend modules.
Override (catalogue)
A saved change applied on top of a built-in catalogue product. Where nothing is overridden, the base value continues to apply.
Paranoid / soft delete
Most MatrixIQ tables hide a deleted record from lists and reports while keeping it recoverable.
Privileged
A flag on a role granting broad, organisation-wide reach. Super Admin, Admin and Manager carry it. Several sensitive modules deliberately refuse it.
Product key
The identifier linking a catalogue override to its base product, and an accessory to its parent. Not a display name.
Role key
The stable internal identifier of a role, such as user for the Sales role. Permission checks use the key, so a role can be relabelled safely.
Section
The sidebar group a module appears under. Reordering in Module Manager happens within a section.
Sweep (daily report)
The scheduled job that checks who has filed, sends reminders and raises missed-report warnings. Safe to run manually and safe to repeat.
Dry run
A mode in which the daily report sweep computes and logs everything but sends nothing.
