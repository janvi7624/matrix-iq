02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This SOP explains how the Back Office prepares, dispatches, receives back and closes Delivery Challans for demonstration equipment, and how the technical support desk triages, assigns, works and resolves after-sales tickets against service targets.

Both modules sit at the physical and post-sale ends of the business: one controls equipment leaving and returning to the premises, the other controls the company's response once a customer has a problem. Both are entered through work that starts elsewhere, so this document also sets out the handoffs in and out.

03Introduction

3.1 What the Operations modules do

Back Office answers "where is our equipment, and did it come back in one piece". Technical Support answers "a customer has a problem — who owns it, and are we meeting our targets".

Back Office Operations issues a Delivery Challan whenever equipment leaves the premises for a customer demonstration. The challan records what went out, with serial numbers; it is dispatched; and when the equipment returns it is checked against a verification checklist covering condition, accessories, missing items and serial numbers. Only then is the challan closed, with the cost of the demonstration recorded. A monthly summary rolls the whole thing up by sales person.

Technical Support takes after-sales tickets raised against a project or a quotation, triages them to a priority, stamps service-level targets, assigns an engineer, and carries the conversation with the reporter through to resolution and closure. A thirteen-status lifecycle makes it explicit at every point who is holding the ticket.

3.2 Why they exist

  • Equipment that leaves must come back. A challan with serial numbers and an expected return date turns an informal loan into a tracked movement.
  • Damage must be recorded when it is found. The return checklist captures condition, missing accessories and serial mismatches at the moment of receipt, not from memory weeks later.
  • Demonstration cost should be visible. Closing a challan asks what the demonstration cost, and the monthly summary reports it by sales person.
  • A support promise should be measurable. Response and resolution targets are set by priority, and breach is judged live rather than from a flag someone forgot to update.
  • Nobody should mark their own homework. An engineer cannot close a ticket — closure follows the customer's confirmation or a manager's decision.

An important subtlety about the Manager role

The Back Office module tile is visible to the generic manager role. Acting on a challan is narrower: a manager qualifies only if they are listed as a manager of the Back Office department. A Sales or Technical manager can therefore see the module but will find the actions refused. 06Detailed Step-by-Step Procedures

6.1 Generating a Delivery Challan from a demo request

Preconditions

  • You are an authorised Back Office actor (§4.1).
  • The demo request is an on-site demo and has cleared both technical approval and manager approval — its status is Pending Back Office.
  • The equipment is physically available.

Procedure

  1. Open Operations → Back Office Operations, on the All Delivery Challans view.
  2. Find the demo request awaiting a challan and select Generate DC.
  3. The Generate Delivery Challan panel opens, pre-filled with the products the demo request listed. If the request listed none, the panel says so — "No products were listed as required on this request" — and you should go back to the requester rather than guessing.
  4. Set the Expected return date. This is what makes an overdue return visible later.
  5. Select Create Delivery Challan.

What happens next

  • A challan is created with the next number in the configured series — the prefix comes from Administration → Application Settings → Number Series.
  • The challan's status is Prepared.
  • The demo request moves to DC Generated, so Sales can see the handover happened.
  • An audit entry is written.

6.2 Creating a manual Delivery Challan

Use this where equipment must go out without a demo request behind it — an exhibition, a site survey, a replacement part.

  1. Select Create Manual DC.
  2. Optionally Link to a project. Doing so prefills the client details. Where no project exists, type a free-text project label instead — for example a project not yet in the system.
  3. Enter Client name *, and the Client phone and Client address where known.
  4. Enter Requested By — who asked for the equipment. Pick a name, or choose Other (type a name)… and type it.
  5. Set the Expected return date.
  6. Add the Items. Each line takes:
    ColumnRequiredNotes
    ProductRequiredWhat is going out.
    HSN CodeOptionalLabelled optional on screen; nothing validates it.
    Serial NumberOptional at this pointRecord it before dispatch — see the warning below.
    QuantityRequired—
    PriceOptionalSet by Back Office only. It appears on the generated PDF.
  7. Select Create Delivery Challan, or Cancel.
Serial numbers are not enforced — but record them anyway

Nothing in MatrixIQ requires a serial number. The return verification nevertheless asks whether the serial number was verified, and a wrong serial number is one of the standard return remarks. Without a serial recorded at dispatch, neither question can be answered honestly. Treat recording serials before dispatch as mandatory in practice.

6.3 Dispatching the material

Preconditions

  • The challan is at Prepared.
  • The physical goods match the item lines.
  • Serial numbers have been captured.

Procedure

  1. Open the challan.
  2. Check each item line against the physical goods, correcting quantity or product if needed.
  3. Enter the serial numbers and select Save Serial Numbers.
  4. Produce the paperwork — Download PDF or Print DC — and send it with the goods.
  5. Select Dispatch Material.

What happens next

  • The challan moves to Dispatched.
  • The linked demo request moves to Material Dispatched.
  • An audit entry is written.

6.4 Verifying and receiving the return

Important — "Verified" is an action, not a status

The MatrixIQ Feature Reference describes the challan chain as prepared → dispatched → returned → verified → closed. The application has four challan statuses: Prepared, Dispatched, Returned, Closed. There is no separate "Verified" status.

Verification is the action that moves a challan from Dispatched to Returned: the button is Verify & Receive Material, and completing the checklist is what records the return. So "returned" in MatrixIQ already means "returned and verified".

Preconditions

  • The challan is at Dispatched.
  • The equipment is physically back, in front of you.

Procedure

  1. Open the challan. The Material return — verification checklist appears.
  2. Record the Condition: Good, Minor damage or Major damage.
  3. Set the Flags — whether anything is missing and whether anything is damaged.
  4. Tick the Accessories returned: power cable, remote, adapter, stand, packing. Leave unticked whatever did not come back.
  5. Confirm whether the serial number was verified against what went out.
  6. Choose any standard remark tags that apply:
    TagUse when
    Good conditionEverything came back as it went out.
    Minor scratchCosmetic damage only.
    Major damageFunctional or significant damage.
    Adapter missingAn accessory did not come back.
    Power cable missing
    Wrong serial numberA different unit came back from the one sent.
    Packing damagedThe packaging is no longer reusable.
    CustomAnything else — describe it in Remarks.
  7. Add Remarks — the field invites "Additional detail…". Write what a colleague would need to know in three months.
  8. Select Verify & Receive Material.

What happens next

  • The challan moves to Returned, and the verification record is displayed thereafter as Return verification with Condition, Missing, Damaged, Serial verified and Remarks.
  • The linked demo request moves to Material Returned.
  • An audit entry is written.
Record what you actually found

This checklist is the company's only contemporaneous record of the condition the equipment came back in. If something is missing or damaged, say so here — even when it is awkward, and even when it will be resolved informally. A challan closed as "good condition" with a damaged unit in the store is worse than useless.

6.5 Closing the challan

Preconditions

  • The challan is at Returned.
  • Any damage or shortfall has been dealt with, or at least recorded and escalated.
  • You know whether the demonstration cost anything.

Procedure

  1. Open the challan and select Close DC.
  2. MatrixIQ asks: "Did anything cost money during this demo?" If so, enter the Amount (₹).
  3. Read the warning on the dialog — "Closing finalises this Delivery Challan and cannot be undone" — then confirm, or Cancel.
Leaving the cost blank is a real answer

An empty demonstration cost means nothing was recorded, which is deliberately different from a recorded zero. Entering 0 says someone was asked and answered zero. Both appear in the monthly summary, where the figure shows as a dash when no cost was recorded on any challan in the period.

What happens next

  • The challan moves to Closed and is shown thereafter as a Closed — final record, with the client, address, phone, requested-by, expected return, condition and demonstration cost summarised.
  • The linked demo request moves to DC Closed, completing the demo pipeline.
  • An audit entry is written.
Closing cannot be undone

There is no re-open. Complete the return verification properly and settle any dispute before closing. If a challan is closed in error, record the facts and escalate; the correction is not made from this screen. Escalation route: to be confirmed.

6.6 The monthly summary

  1. Switch to the Summary view.
  2. Read the three tiles:
    TileMeaning
    Total Demos (DCs)How many challans the period covers.
    Total ExpenseThe demonstration costs recorded at closure. Shows a dash when none were recorded.
    Sales PeopleHow many distinct sales people the challans are attributed to.
  3. Read By Sales Person for the breakdown. Challans with no attributable sales person are grouped as unattributed.
  4. Read Monthly Summary for the month-by-month trend.
  5. If the list says "No Delivery Challans in this period", there are none — the run did not fail.

6.7 Raising and triaging a support ticket

6.7.1 Raising a ticket

  1. Open Support → Technical Support and start a new ticket.
  2. Enter a Title * and a Description of the issue *.
  3. Record what is known about the installation: Product, Model / serial number, Customer, Installation site, Contact person, Date the issue occurred, and the Issue type.
  4. Link the Related project where one exists — this is how a ticket is traced back to the deal and the quotation it came from.
  5. State the business impact, which sets the initial priority:
    ImpactPriority
    Low — minor inconvenienceLow
    Medium — partially impairedMedium (the default)
    High — major function unusableHigh
    Critical — system downCritical
  6. Submit. The ticket enters the queue as New.

6.7.2 Triage

Preconditions

  • You are a Manager actor, or an Engineer actor moving a ticket into triage.
  1. Open the queue. Use the three tiles to decide where to start — SLA breached first, then Unassigned.
  2. Open the ticket and read the issue, the customer, the product and the installation details.
  3. Confirm or correct the Priority. This is the single most consequential triage decision, because it sets the targets.
  4. Set the department where appropriate.
  5. Assign to an engineer. An unassigned ticket shows as Unassigned and sits in the pool.
  6. Select Apply.
Targets are stamped at triage

The response and resolution due timestamps are recorded when the ticket is triaged, from the policy in force at that moment — because they are a commitment made at a point in time. Re-deriving them later from a policy that had since been edited would retroactively change what was promised.

Changing a ticket's priority at triage does re-stamp the targets, so a ticket upgraded to Critical is judged against the Critical policy rather than the one it arrived under.

6.7.3 The service-level targets

Response and resolution targets by priority, as configured
PriorityFirst responseResolutionPauses while awaiting the reporter?
Critical30 minutes4 hoursYes
High2 hours8 hoursYes
Medium4 hours24 hoursYes
Low8 hours72 hoursYes

All four policies are configured to run against the clock continuously rather than only during business hours.

Breach is computed, never stored

MatrixIQ does not keep a "breached" flag. It works the answer out every time a ticket is read, from the stamped due timestamps, the status history and the time spent waiting on the reporter. That means the figure is never stale — but it also means the SLA state can change without anyone touching the ticket, simply because the clock passed a threshold.

The practical consequence: check the SLA breached tile at the start of every shift. A ticket nobody has touched can have breached overnight.

Awaiting the reporter stops the resolution clock

Time a ticket spends in Awaiting your information is deducted, and the resolution deadline shifts out by that amount. This is why moving a genuinely blocked ticket into that status — rather than leaving it in progress — matters: it records the truth and stops the company being judged against time it was not in control of. Note that it requires a reason.

6.8 Working, resolving and closing a ticket

6.8.1 Picking up and acknowledging

  1. An engineer finds the ticket in their assignments, or in the unassigned pool.
  2. Open it. Read the issue, the customer and installation details, and the conversation so far.
  3. Move it to Acknowledged or straight to In progress — use the Move to control and select Apply.
  4. Post the first reply in the Conversation thread. This is what satisfies the first-response target.

6.8.2 Working the ticket

SituationMove it toNote
You are actively working on itIn progress—
You need something from the reporterAwaiting your informationA reason is required. Stops the resolution clock.
You need something from a colleague or supplierAwaiting internal teamDoes not stop the clock — it is our delay.
It needs more authority or expertiseEscalatedA reason is required.
The reporter has answeredIn progressThe reporter can make this move themselves by replying.

Use the Conversation thread throughout and Post each update. The Status history below it records every move with when, from and to.

6.8.3 Resolving

  1. When the problem is fixed, move the ticket to Resolved.
  2. Complete the resolution record. These fields are what make a ticket useful to the next engineer:
    • Resolution summary — what was done.
    • Root cause — why it happened.
    • Corrective action — what prevents a recurrence.
  3. Enter the Reason * — resolving requires one.
  4. Select Apply.
Only an engineer or a manager may declare a ticket resolved

Declaring a technical fix is an engineering statement. A reporter — including a channel partner — cannot move a ticket to Resolved. They can only confirm or dispute one.

6.8.4 Closing

Who closesWhen
The reporter (including a partner)They confirm the fix by closing the resolved ticket. This is the normal path.
A Manager actorWhere the customer is unresponsive, or nobody will confirm. A deliberate decision, not a default.
An Engineer actorNever. Otherwise the team marks its own homework and the reopen rate stops meaning anything.

If the fix did not work, the reporter moves the ticket to Reopened instead — a reason is required — and it returns to the desk. A manager may also reopen a ticket that has already been closed.

6.8.5 Cancelling

Cancelling is a Manager actor action, available only from the early statuses — New, Open, Under triage, Assigned, Acknowledged, In progress and the two waiting states. A ticket genuinely being worked is resolved or closed, never cancelled, so the trail shows what actually happened. A reason is required. A manager can later move a cancelled ticket back to Open.

A significant point the reference summary omits

A demo request carries a mode: on-site or virtual. A virtual demo is given over a screen share — nothing leaves the office, so no challan, dispatch or return applies. On manager approval it lands at Ready — Virtual Demo and waits for its date, and the pipeline ends when the demonstration is done.

Virtual demos never reach the Back Office list and must not be challaned. If one appears to, check the mode on the request.

9.1 Search and filtering

  • Back Office — the challan list can be opened directly on a specific challan or demo request by link, which is how drill-downs from performance dashboards arrive. A link to a specific challan always opens the Delivery Challans view, never the Summary.
  • Technical Support — search matches ticket, partner, customer and product. Tickets are badged Internal or by partner, and Unassigned where nobody has picked them up.

9.2 How to interpret the monthly summary

  • Total Expense counts only costs actually recorded at closure. A dash means no challan in the period recorded one — not that demonstrations were free.
  • Challans that cannot be attributed to a sales person are grouped as unattributed. A large unattributed figure usually means manual challans were created without a linked project.
  • The summary counts challans, not demonstrations. A virtual demo never appears, because it never produces a challan.

12.2 Recommended practice

Back Office — accurate records

  • Record serial numbers before every dispatch. MatrixIQ does not insist; the return verification depends on it entirely.
  • Set a realistic expected return date on every challan. It is the only mechanism that surfaces an overdue return.
  • Link a project on a manual challan wherever one exists — it is what attributes the challan in the summary.
  • Record the demonstration cost at closure, entering an explicit zero rather than leaving it blank.

Back Office — the physical handover

  • Check the goods against the item lines before dispatching, not after.
  • Send the printed challan with the goods. It is what the receiving site signs against.
  • Complete the return checklist in front of the equipment, not from memory later.
  • Escalate damage or shortfall before closing. Closing is final.

Support — timeliness

  • Start every shift on the SLA breached tile, then Unassigned.
  • Post a substantive first reply within the response target. A holding message that says nothing is not a response.
  • Move a genuinely blocked ticket to Awaiting your information rather than leaving it in progress — it is both more honest and stops the clock.
  • Triage to the right priority. The priority is the promise.

Support — useful records

  • Always complete the resolution summary, root cause and corrective action. The next engineer with the same fault depends on it.
  • Keep the conversation in the ticket thread, not in e-mail or chat.
  • Write reasons that will still make sense in six months.
  • Link the related project so the ticket can be traced to the deal and the quotation.

Confidentiality and access control

  • Challans carry client names, addresses and equipment values. Do not circulate them outside the people who need them.
  • Tickets carry customer site details and contact names. Treat them as customer confidential.
  • Never share an account. Every action is attributed to the signed-in user and the originating address.
  • Remember that a partner reads the conversation thread on their own tickets. Keep internal speculation out of it.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Check whether a challan already exists for a demo request before creating a manual one.
  • Search the support queue before raising a ticket — the same fault may already be open.
  • Reopen an existing ticket rather than raising a second one for a recurrence; the reopen rate is a real measure.

Keeping an auditable history

  • Record events when they happen. A backdated entry with a note is better than a silent gap, but worse than a timely one.
  • Use the status vocabulary honestly — a ticket parked in progress while nobody works it misreports the company's performance.
  • Never work around a refusal. If MatrixIQ will not let you do something, the rule in §12.1 is the reason.

14Glossary and Appendix

14.1 Glossary

Actor kind
How the support workflow classifies a viewer: Manager, Engineer or Partner. Several roles map onto one kind, and some roles map onto none.
Back Office actor
Someone authorised to act on a Delivery Challan: the Back Office role, Admin, Super Admin, or the Back Office department's configured manager.
Breach
A missed response or resolution target. Computed live from the stamped deadlines and the status history, never stored.
Delivery Challan (DC)
The document recording equipment leaving the premises and coming back. Four statuses: Prepared, Dispatched, Returned, Closed.
Demo mode
On-site or virtual. A virtual demo involves no equipment movement and never produces a challan.
Demo outcome
What the demonstration achieved — successful, needs follow-up, pending decision or cancelled. Recorded separately from the pipeline status.
Escalation
A support status indicating the ticket needs more authority or expertise. Requires a reason.
Manual challan
A challan created directly by Back Office with no demo request behind it.
Pause (SLA)
Time a ticket spends awaiting the reporter, deducted from the resolution clock.
Return verification
The checklist completed when equipment comes back: condition, flags, accessories, serial verification, remark tags and remarks. Completing it is what moves a challan to Returned.
Service-level target
The response and resolution time promised for a priority. Stamped onto the ticket at triage.
Triage
The act of confirming priority, setting department and assigning. It is also when the service targets are stamped.
TMS
Technical Project Management — technical projects, tasks, bill of materials and procurement. A connected module, not part of Operations.
Unassigned pool
Tickets nobody has picked up. Visible to engineers so incoming work can be claimed.
