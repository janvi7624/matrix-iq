02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This SOP explains how the Accounts team uses MatrixIQ to see, check, hold and settle every payment the company owes — and how to read the audit trail behind those actions. It is written so that an Accounts user can work the queue end to end without asking for technical help, and so that colleagues in HR, Administration and the Technical team understand exactly where their responsibility ends and the Accounts team's begins.

  • Secondary — Super Admin and Admin accounts, who hold the same access plus the audit trail.
  • Reference — HR, Administration and Technical colleagues who need to know what reaches Accounts and when.

2.5 Scope

In scope. Everything an Accounts user does inside MatrixIQ: reading the queue, opening a payment, checking its origin, placing and lifting holds, recording payment, exporting, reading the month-by-employee reimbursement summary, and — for Super Admin and Admin only — reading the Audit Log.

Also in scope, as context. The upstream approval chains that put work into the Accounts queue, described only as far as is needed to understand what Accounts receives and what it must refuse.

2.6 Limitations

  • This document does not describe bank operations, statutory filing, tax computation or any accounting system outside MatrixIQ. MatrixIQ records that a payment was made; it is not a ledger.
  • It does not describe how to create the records that become payments. Those belong to the HR, Administration, Operations and Technical Project Management SOPs.
  • Role names, module visibility and some approval routings are configuration, not code. A change made in Administration can alter what is described here without any notice to this document.
  • Details the application could not confirm are marked To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application and must not be treated as fact.

03Introduction

3.1 What the Accounts module does

Five different parts of MatrixIQ can end in the company paying money. The Accounts module gathers all five into one queue, so that the Accounts team has a single place to look instead of five.

Before the Accounts module existed, an approved reimbursement sheet sat in the Reimbursement screen, an approved travel request sat in Travel Schedule, a finance-approved bill of materials sat in the Technical Project Management screens, and office and admin spend sat in their own registers. Each had its own idea of "ready to pay". The Payments module reads all five and presents them as one list of payable items with one shared vocabulary: who is owed, how much, what for, who approved it, when it is due, and whether it has been paid.

The important design point

The Payment Queue is assembled when you open it, from the five source records. It is not a separate copy of them. When you mark something paid here, MatrixIQ writes that fact back onto the original record — the reimbursement sheet, the BOM request, the travel request or the expense row. There is no second set of books to reconcile, and nothing can drift out of step.

3.2 Why it exists

  • One queue, not five. Nothing approved anywhere in MatrixIQ can be forgotten simply because nobody thought to open that screen.
  • A visible due date. Every payable item carries a target date, so "what am I late on" is the first question the screen answers.
  • A legitimate way to pause. When a bank detail is missing or an amount looks wrong, the item can be put on hold with a written reason instead of being silently skipped.
  • An auditable record. Every status-changing action — including every hold, resume and payment — is appended to the Audit Log with the user, the time and the originating IP address.

3.3 Business processes it supports

3.4 Who should use it

The Payment Queue is for the Accounts team and for the organisation's administrators. It is deliberately not available to every manager in the company. Holding a manager role elsewhere — in Sales, in Operations, in the Technical team — does not grant sight of the company's payment queue.

04Access and Permissions

4.1 Who can open the Payment Queue

Access to Payments is granted in two independent ways. A user qualifies if either applies.

By configuration — Accounts department managerany roleWhoever is listed as a manager of the department named Accounts in Department Master qualifies regardless of their role. This is a deliberate, configured grant.
Holding a manager role is not enough

The generic manager role does not open the Payment Queue. A Sales manager, an Operations manager or a Technical manager cannot see the company's payments simply because they manage a team. The only manager who qualifies is the one actually listed as a manager of the Accounts department. To remove that access, remove the person from that department's managers in Department Master.

Hiding the tile is not the control

The same check guards the screen and the data behind it. Typing the address /accounts/payments directly does not work for an unauthorised account — MatrixIQ returns them to the dashboard. If Administration disables the Payments module in Module Manager, the screen and its interfaces close together for everyone.

4.4 Which records are visible

Within the Payment Queue there is no further scoping. An authorised Accounts user sees every payable item from every department, because that is what a company-wide payment queue is. Department and employee filters exist to narrow the view, never to restrict it.

4.5 Partner separation

External channel partners have no path to this module at all. Partner accounts are confined to the Partner Portal and are refused everywhere else, including every Accounts screen and interface. Likewise no internal account, however senior, can enter the Partner Portal. There is no "view as partner" mode.

06Detailed Step-by-Step Procedures

Every procedure below states what must be true before you start, what to do, what happens afterwards, and what to do when it does not work.

6.1 Opening and reading the Payment Queue

Preconditions

  • You are signed in to MatrixIQ with an Accounts, Super Admin or Admin account, or you are listed as a manager of the Accounts department.
  • The Payments module is enabled in Module Manager.

Procedure

  1. Open the sidebar and expand the Accounts section.
  2. Select Payments. The screen opens on the Payment Queue tab.
  3. Read the four tiles across the top — Pending, Due Today, Overdue, Paid This Month. These are totals for the whole company and are not affected by the filters below them.
  4. Read the list. It is sorted so that the most urgent work is first: overdue items, then items due today or imminently, then items due within about three days, then everything else. Within each group the oldest item comes first.
  5. Narrow the list if you need to, using the toolbar:
    • Search — matches the payee, the description, the requester, the source label and the payment reference.
    • All Sources — restrict to one of the five sources.
    • All Statuses — Payment Required, On Hold, or all.
    • Period — Any time, Month, Quarter, Half-Year or Full Year, together with a Financial Year and the specific period.
    • Department and Employee — both lists are built from the payments themselves, so a choice that could only ever return nothing is never offered. The Employee filter matches both the payee and the requester.
    • Amount (₹) — a minimum, a maximum, or both. An empty box means "no bound", not zero.
    • Overdue only — the "what am I late on" view.
  6. Use Clear filters to return to the full queue. The toolbar shows how many filters are currently active.
Which date a filter measures

On the Payment Queue tab, period and date filters are measured against the date the item was raised — the queue is about work arriving. On the Payment History tab they are measured against the date it was paid — history is about money leaving. The same filter therefore answers a different, and correct, question on each tab.

6.2 Reviewing one payment before acting

Never pay from the list row alone. Open the item first.

  1. Select the row. The detail panel opens.
  2. Check the header facts: Amount, Payee, Description, Requested By, Approved By, and the Source.
  3. Select Open Original Request to open the record this payment came from, in its own module. Use this whenever the description alone is not enough:
    • Reimbursement → the Reimbursement screen
    • Admin Expense → the Admin Expenses screen
    • Office Operation Expense → the Office Operation Expenses register
    • BOM Request → the Technical Project Management BOM Requests screen
    • Travel Booking → the Travel Schedule screen
  4. For an Admin Expense or an Office Operation Expense, the panel lists the individual entries that make up the month's sheet. Read them before paying — this is the only place the composition of a monthly sheet is visible.
  5. Confirm that the amount you are about to pay is the amount you have just read. See the warning below.
A monthly sheet can grow while you are looking at it

An Admin Expense or Office Operation Expense sheet is "every unpaid entry for that month". If a colleague logs another entry for the same month while your screen is open, the sheet's real total changes. MatrixIQ guards against this: it records the total you were shown and refuses the payment if the figure has moved. If that happens, refresh and read the entries again before paying.

6.3 Marking a payment as paid

Preconditions

  • The item's status is Payment Required. An item that is on hold must be resumed first.
  • The underlying record is in the status that makes it payable — see the table in §8.3.
  • The money has actually moved, or you are recording it at the moment it moves.

Procedure

  1. Open the payment (§6.2) and select Pay from the list row, or Make Payment from the detail panel.
  2. The Confirm Payment dialog opens, restating the Payee, the Amount and the Method. Read them.
  3. Enter the Payment date. This field is required. The on-screen guidance is explicit: "The day the money actually moved — not necessarily today." Back-date it when you are recording a payment made earlier.
  4. Enter the payment Reference where the source asks for one — a transaction ID, UTR or cheque number.
  5. Attach proof where required. For a BOM Request this is mandatory: MatrixIQ refuses the payment with "Payment proof is required for a BOM request payment" if nothing is attached.
  6. Add Remarks if anything needs explaining.
  7. Answer the confirmation — "Are you sure this payment has been completed?" — by selecting Confirm Payment. Back returns without recording anything.

What happens next

  • The item moves from the Payment Queue to Payment History with status Paid and the payment date you entered.
  • The source record is updated in its own module — a reimbursement sheet becomes Payment Completed, a travel request moves to Ticket Booked and is handed to HR for final verification, and so on.
  • An audit entry is appended recording who paid, when, the previous and new status, and the originating IP address.
  • The Paid This Month tile increases.

If it will not complete

MessageWhat it means and what to do

"This request is not awaiting payment"The BOM request has not been finance-approved. It is not yours to settle yet.
"This request is not ready for booking/payment"The travel request has not been approved by Administration.

6.4 Putting a payment on hold

Use a hold when something is genuinely wrong or missing. A hold is visible to everyone who can see the queue, and it tells the requester why — which is what makes it better than quietly skipping the row.

  1. Open the payment and select Put On Hold.
  2. Enter a Reason for Hold. This is required. Write what is actually missing — the field's own examples are missing bank details, invoice missing and amount mismatch.
  3. Select Put On Hold to confirm, or Cancel to abandon.

What happens next

  • The item's status becomes On Hold. It stays in the Payment Queue and continues to count towards the Pending total — a hold suspends payment, not the obligation.
  • The person who raised the underlying record is notified. An Office Operation Expense monthly sheet may have several contributors, and each is notified. An Admin Expense sheet has no single requester, so no requester notification is sent for that source.
  • An audit entry is appended.
A hold does not change the source record

Holding a payment lays a marker over the item in the Accounts queue. The reimbursement sheet, BOM request or travel request keeps its own status untouched, so the upstream module still reads correctly. This also means a hold can only be lifted here, in Accounts.

If it will not complete

  • "This payment is already paid" — a settled payment cannot be held. If the payment was wrong, raise it with the Document Owner and the originating department; the correction is not made from this screen.
  • "This payment is already on hold" — someone has just held it. Refresh to see the existing hold and its reason.

6.5 Resuming a held payment

  1. Find the item — set the status filter to On Hold to list every held payment at once.
  2. Open it and read the hold reason. Confirm that the problem is genuinely resolved.
  3. Select Resume Payment.
  4. The status returns to Payment Required. Pay it in the normal way (§6.3) once you are ready.

If MatrixIQ reports "No active hold found for this payment", the hold has already been lifted by a colleague. Refresh the screen.

6.6 Payment History and the CSV export

  1. Select the Payment History tab. It lists settled payments, with a Paid Date column in place of Due Date.
  2. Apply filters exactly as on the queue. Remember that here they measure the paid date.
  3. Select the export control to download the current view as a CSV file.
The export always matches the screen

The queue, the history and the CSV export all run through the same filtering logic, so an export can never disagree with the list that produced it. If the CSV looks wrong, the filters on screen are the thing to check.

6.7 The Reimbursement Report

This tab answers a question the queue cannot: how much reimbursement spend belongs to each month, and how much of it has actually been paid.

  1. Select the Reimbursement Report tab.
  2. Read the table. It has one row per month and shows Month, Total Expense and Paid, broken down by employee.
  3. Select Export CSV to download it.

How to read the figures

  • Total Expense is every reimbursement claim dated in that month, whatever stage it has reached.
  • Paid counts only months whose sheet has reached Payment Completed. A month that is approved but not yet settled therefore shows a total with nothing against Paid — that is the gap the Accounts queue exists to close.
  • Admin Expense entries are excluded. They share the same underlying table but carry their own approval and payment state and appear in the queue in their own right. Including them here would double-count them.
  • If the screen says "No reimbursements recorded yet", no claims exist for the period, not that the report failed.

6.8 Reading the Audit Log

Super Admin and Admin only

This procedure cannot be performed by an Accounts account. An Accounts user who needs an audit extract must ask a Super Admin or Admin.

  1. Open the sidebar section Administration and select Audit Log.
  2. Narrow the trail with the filters:
    • Search — free text across the user, action, remark, previous and new status, and IP address.
    • Entity — a fixed list of record types, including Reimbursement Sheets, Travel Schedule, TMS BOM Requests, Delivery Challans, Demo requests, Leads, Quotations, Projects, Marketing Requests, Departments, User Imports, TMS Projects, TMS Tasks, Tasks, Attendance, Leave Requests, Employee Exits and Custom Modules.
    • User and Role — both built from the trail itself, so an option that would return nothing is never offered.
    • From and To — inclusive of both days.
  3. Read the eight columns: Date/Time, User, Role, Entity, Action, Previous → New, Remarks and IP.
  4. Page through the results. Entries are shown newest first, fifty to a page.
  5. Select Export page as PDF to keep a copy of the page currently displayed, or Refresh to reload.
Two limits worth knowing

The Entity dropdown is narrower than the trail. MatrixIQ records audit entries against more record types than the dropdown lists — office operation expenses, daily reports, leave claims, materials, support tickets, partners and others. Those entries exist and are returned by an unfiltered search, but cannot be isolated from the dropdown. Use the free-text Search box to reach them.

The export covers the page on screen, not the whole result set. Whether a full-result export exists is To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.

Why reimbursements usually show as overdue

A reimbursement sheet's target date is the 20th of the month the expenses belong to — not three days after HR approved it. Because a month's sheet is normally approved during the following month, most sheets are already past that date when they reach Accounts. This is the intended behaviour of a fixed pay date, not a fault. Judge urgency by the figures, not by the red badge alone.

Do not read Billing as an instruction to pay

Nothing in Billing is payable and nothing in it ever reaches the Payment Queue. Treat it as a commercial report. Its figures are deliberately not added together across the three bases, because a deal's estimate, quoted total and purchase order amount routinely differ.

8.4 How the due date is worked out

The due date is a target, not a promise recorded on the record

None of the five sources carries a real due-date field. MatrixIQ computes one so that urgency can be shown and the queue can be ordered. It is labelled as a target in the interface and must not be quoted to anyone as a contractual date.

10.2 How notifications work

  • In-app notifications appear in the bell in the MatrixIQ header. They are shared by every module and are append-only.
  • E-mail is sent through Amazon SES and is configuration-dependent. If the platform's e-mail settings have not been provisioned, MatrixIQ records the intent and skips the send without failing the business action.
Do not rely on e-mail alone

Because an unconfigured mail service fails quietly, the queue itself is the authoritative work list. Open the Payment Queue daily. Never conclude that nothing is outstanding because no e-mail arrived.

12.1 Mandatory MatrixIQ rules

These are enforced by the platform. They are not matters of judgement.

12.2 Recommended practice

These are recommendations from this SOP, not platform rules.

Accurate data entry

  • Enter the real payment date. Defaulting to today quietly corrupts every period report.
  • Record a reference that can be traced in the bank statement — the UTR, transaction ID or cheque number — not a free-text note.
  • Keep remarks factual. They are read by people outside Accounts and are preserved in the audit trail.

Supporting documents

  • Attach proof even where MatrixIQ does not insist on it. Only the BOM request stage requires it; attaching it everywhere makes a later query answerable without leaving the platform.
  • Before paying, open the original request at least once. The queue row is a summary, not evidence.

Timeliness

  • Work the queue in its default order. It is already sorted by urgency.
  • Clear the Overdue only view before anything else each day.
  • Review held payments at a fixed point each week. A hold with a resolved reason that nobody resumed is an invisible delay.

Confidentiality and access control

  • The queue carries company-wide salary-adjacent and personal expense information. Do not export it to shared drives or send it outside the Accounts team without the Document Owner's agreement.
  • Never share an account. Every action is attributed to the signed-in user and the originating IP address; a shared login destroys that attribution.
  • Partner-facing systems must never receive an Accounts export. Partners have no visibility of internal spend.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Check Payment History before re-attempting any payment that reported an error.
  • If two people work the queue, agree a split — by source or by department — rather than both working from the top.

Keeping the history auditable

  • Use holds instead of skipping rows. A skipped row leaves no trace; a hold leaves a reason.
  • Never correct a mistake by paying a second time. Record the facts and escalate.

14Glossary and Appendix

14.1 Glossary

Accounts department manager
Whoever is listed as a manager of the department named Accounts in Department Master. This configuration grants payment-queue access regardless of the person's role.
Admin Expense
A company-paid cost such as a hotel, ticket or visa fee, recorded centrally and often split across several employees.
Append-only
A record that can be added to but never edited or deleted. The Audit Log and the notification ledger are append-only.
Audit Log
The platform-wide trail of status-changing actions. Readable by Super Admin and Admin only.
Billing
A separate, read-only view of won business and its value. It is about revenue, not spend, and carries no payment actions.
BOM Request
A bill of materials request raised by the Technical team, approved through Technical, Administration and Finance stages before it becomes payable.
Delivery Challan
The Back Office document that records equipment leaving and returning. It is not a payment document.
Due date
In this module, a target date computed when the screen is read. It is never a stored commitment.
Hold
A marker placed over a payment that blocks settlement without changing the source record's own status. Requires a written reason.
Module Manager
The Administration screen that enables, disables, renames and reorders modules and controls which roles see them. Disabling a module closes its data interfaces as well as hiding the tile.
Office Operation Expense
Routine office running costs recorded in the HR / Administration register.
Paranoid / soft delete
Most MatrixIQ tables hide a deleted record from lists and reports while keeping it recoverable. The office expense register is an exception — a deletion there is permanent.
Payee
The person or party being paid.
Payment Queue
The assembled list of everything unpaid across all five sources.
Payment source
Which module raised a payment. One of five: Reimbursement, Admin Expense, Office Operation Expense, BOM Request, Travel Booking.
Privileged
A flag on a role that grants broad, organisation-wide reach. Super Admin, Admin and Manager carry it. It is deliberately not enough on its own to open the Payment Queue.
Reimbursement sheet
One employee's claims for one calendar month, approved and paid as a single unit.
Requester
Who raised the record that became a payment. Not always the payee.
Sheet (monthly)
For Admin and Office expenses, all qualifying entries for one calendar month grouped into a single payable item.
