02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This guide explains how to use the MatrixIQ Partner Portal: how to send us an opportunity, follow it as our sales team works on it, request a demonstration for your customer, find and download quotations and marketing material, ask our marketing team for collateral, and raise after-sales support.

It is written so that you can use every part of the portal confidently without needing to call us — and so that you know, at each point, what we will do next and roughly when.

03Introduction

3.1 What the Partner Portal is

The Partner Portal is your own view of the work we are doing together. It is not a separate system holding a copy of your information — it is a window onto the very same records our sales, technical and marketing teams work in.

That matters in a practical way. When you submit an opportunity it enters our internal sales pipeline directly, so there is no re-keying, no delay while somebody copies it across, and no risk of the two versions disagreeing. When our team updates it, you see the change. When we share a quotation with you, it is the same document our team prepared.

3.4 A note on how your records are kept separate

Your information is yours

Every screen in the portal is bound to your partner company. You see your own submissions, your own demonstrations, the quotations we have shared with you, your own marketing requests and your own support tickets — and nothing else.

The separation works in both directions: your portal account can only reach partner pages, and our internal staff cannot enter the Partner Portal at all. There is no "view as partner" mode. When a colleague of ours helps you with something, they are looking at the internal record, not at your screen.

4.4 Mandatory access and security rules

Please read and follow these — they are conditions of your portal access
  1. Use your own account. Every action is recorded against the person signed in. Sharing a login makes it impossible to tell who did what — which protects nobody, least of all you.
  2. Do not try to reach pages outside the portal. Anything outside the partner area is for our internal staff. Attempting to reach it simply returns you to your Dashboard, and the attempt is recorded.
  3. You can only see what has been made available to your partner account. If something you expect is missing, ask — do not assume it is a fault.
  4. You cannot revise a quotation we have shared. Ask us for a revised version instead.
  5. Edit a submitted opportunity only when we have asked for changes. That is the one circumstance in which editing is open.
  6. Treat downloaded material as confidential to your business relationship with us, unless it is plainly intended for your customers — brochures, datasheets and case studies are.
  7. Tell us immediately if you think an account has been compromised, or if a colleague leaves and their access should be removed.

06Step-by-Step Procedures

6.1 Signing in for the first time

  1. We create your account and send a welcome e-mail containing your username and a one-time password.
  2. Open the portal address in the e-mail and sign in with those details.
  3. You are taken straight to a password-change screen. You cannot use the portal until you set your own password. This is deliberate — it means nobody at our end knows your password.
  4. Choose a password you do not use anywhere else, and save it in your own password manager.
  5. You arrive at your Dashboard.
If the welcome e-mail never arrives

Check your spam folder first. If it is genuinely missing, ask your relationship manager to resend it — there is a control on our side for exactly this. Please do not ask a colleague to share their login with you in the meantime.

6.2 Your Dashboard

The Dashboard is the first screen you see. It answers two questions: how is our joint business doing, and is anything waiting on me?

6.3 Submitting a new opportunity

Before you start

  • Have the customer's contact details to hand.
  • Know roughly which of our product lines the opportunity involves.
  • Gather any supporting files — a requirement document, a floor plan, a tender notice, an e-mail trail.

Procedure

  1. Open Sales → My Leads / Projects and select Submit an opportunity.
  2. Enter the customer contact details:
    • Client Representative Name — the person at the customer you are dealing with.
    • Company, Phone, Email, Address.
    • Alternate Contact Name (optional) and Alternate Contact Phone (optional) — worth adding; it saves a chase later if the main contact is unavailable.
  3. Describe the opportunity:
    • Opportunity Type.
    • Department — tick every team you think this deal involves. More than one is fine, and it is better to tick one too many than one too few; it decides who inside our business picks it up.
    • Priority — Medium or High.
    • Expected closing date.
    • Approx. Project Price (₹) — your best estimate. It does not commit you to anything.
    • Expected timeline — free text, for example Q3 2026 or within 2 months.
    • Remarks — anything we should know. Competitive position, budget status, who the decision-maker is.
  4. Attach your supporting files.
  5. Select Submit an opportunity, or Cancel to abandon.

What happens next

  • Your submission enters our internal sales pipeline immediately as a real project record, flagged as yours.
  • Our sales team is notified and begins its review.
  • Your submission appears in your list at Awaiting sales review.
A good submission gets a faster answer

The two things that most often slow a review down are a missing decision-maker and an unclear scope. Putting both in the Remarks field at the outset usually saves an entire round of questions.

The position reflects the furthest evidence, not just a label

If a quotation has been shared with you, the position shows Quotation even if the underlying stage has not been updated yet — because a shared quotation is harder evidence of progress than a field someone has not got round to changing. The same applies to a booked demonstration. You are seeing the most accurate reading we have.

6.4.2 When we ask for more information

  1. Your Dashboard flags the submission as needing your action, and its position shows Information requested.
  2. Open it and read our question in the conversation thread.
  3. Reply in the thread. This is the normal way to answer.
  4. If the answer means changing the submission itself — a corrected value, a different contact, an added department — edit the submission. This is the one circumstance in which editing is open.
  5. Once we have what we need, we move it forward and the position changes.
Editing is only open while we have asked for changes

The edit option appears only while the submission is at Information requested. If you need a change at any other time, ask in the conversation thread and we will reopen it. Every change you make is recorded by name and date, which protects both of us.

6.4.3 The conversation thread

Each submission carries a single thread combining our decisions and your replies, in order. You will see entries such as Submitted by partner, More information requested, Accepted by sales and Not taken forward, with your own replies alongside. Our sales reviewer sees exactly the same thread, so nobody has to be told what was already asked.

Keep the conversation in the thread

An answer given by e-mail or over the phone is not attached to the record. Putting it in the thread means the next person who picks up the opportunity — at our end or yours — has the full history.

6.4.4 If we decline

A submission marked Not taken forward carries our reason in the thread. This is not necessarily final: if circumstances change — a budget is confirmed, a specification is clarified — tell us in the thread. A declined submission can be accepted later. An accepted one, however, stays accepted; if an accepted opportunity subsequently fails it is closed through the normal pipeline as Lost or On hold, not by retracting the acceptance.

6.5 Quotations

What this area contains

Every quotation our team has shared with you. Quotations are shared deliberately — a quotation only appears here once we have sent it to you.

Procedure

  1. Open Sales → Quotations. The list is headed Shared with you.
  2. Read the columns: Quotation, Customer, Status, Total and Shared.
  3. Open one to see its detail:
    • Any Note from our team accompanying the share.
    • The line items — Description, Rate and Amount.
    • Subtotal, Discount and Total.
    • Its version history and validity.
  4. Download the PDF. It carries our company header and terms, and is the document to forward to your customer.
Read and download only — by design

You cannot revise a quotation. If the scope, quantities, pricing or validity need to change, ask your relationship manager or reply on the related opportunity. We will prepare a revised version, and it will appear here as a new version alongside the original so the history stays intact.

Check the validity before you forward it

Each quotation carries a validity window. A quotation past its validity may no longer reflect current pricing. If you are close to the date, ask us to confirm or reissue before sending it on.

6.6 Requesting a product demonstration

Before you start

  • Agree a workable date and time with your customer first — and ideally a second option.
  • Know whether it needs to be at the customer's site or can be done online.
  • Know which of our product lines you want shown.

Procedure

  1. Open Sales → Demo Requests and select Request a demo.
  2. Link the Project and, where relevant, the Quotation the demonstration relates to.
  3. Enter Client name *, Company and Location.
  4. Choose How is the demo given?
    OptionWhat it means
    At the client's site — equipment is sentWe ship equipment to the site. This needs more lead time: the equipment has to be prepared, dispatched, demonstrated and returned.
    Online — screen share, nothing shippedConducted remotely. Usually much quicker to arrange, as no equipment movement is involved.
  5. Select the Domain(s) demonstrated — the product lines to be shown.
  6. Enter the Demo date & time * you need, and a Priority — Medium or High.
  7. Describe the Demo objective — what the customer needs to see in order to be convinced — and add any Notes.
  8. Select Request a demonstration.

What happens next

  • Your request reaches the salesperson assigned to your opportunity.
  • They select the technical colleague who will give the demonstration, based on the product and availability. This is not something you choose.
  • The request then goes through our internal technical and management approvals.
  • For an on-site demonstration, our back office prepares and dispatches the equipment, and recovers it afterwards.
  • You follow the whole thing from Your requests, which shows what you Requested for and the Confirmed date once one is agreed.
What you will see as it progresses
StatusMeaning
RequestedSubmitted; nobody has picked it up yet.

Why "Approved — being scheduled" and "Scheduled" are different

Approved means we have agreed to do it and are getting ready. Scheduled means a date is actually fixed. Please do not confirm a date to your customer until you see Scheduled with a confirmed date.

6.7 Marketing Materials

Everything in this area has been published for partners. If it is here, you may use it.

  1. Open Marketing → Marketing Materials. The Library appears.
  2. Browse the folder tree, or select Open folder to go into one. Documents are grouped by product and separated by category — datasheets, case studies, brochures, presentations, company profiles, certificates, and video and image assets.
  3. Use Search the library to find something by name or description.
  4. Open or download any document.
If something you need is not there

The library holds what has been published for partners — not everything that exists. If you need a document you cannot find, raise a Marketing Request (§6.8) or ask your relationship manager. It may simply be that nobody has published it yet.

A note on links

Some items open a document hosted elsewhere rather than downloading a file. If a link does not open for you, tell us — it usually means a permission needs adjusting at our end, not that you have done anything wrong.

6.9 Raising a technical support ticket

Before you start

  • Gather the product and, if you can, its model or serial number.
  • Know the site and the contact person there.
  • Be ready to describe what is actually happening, not only what you think the cause is.

Procedure

  1. Open Support → Technical Support and select Raise a support ticket.
  2. Enter a Title * — a short summary of the problem.
  3. Choose the Issue type.
  4. Choose the Business impact. This is the most important field on the form, because it sets how quickly we must respond:
    Business impactWe respond withinWe aim to resolve within
    Critical — system down30 minutes4 hours
    High — major function unusable2 hours8 hours
    Medium — partially impaired4 hours24 hours
    Low — minor inconvenience8 hours72 hours
  5. Link the Related project where there is one. This connects the ticket to the original deal and quotation and saves us asking.
  6. Record what you know about the installation: Product, Model / serial number, Date the issue occurred, Customer, Installation site and Contact person.
  7. Complete Describe the issue *. Include what happens, when it started, whether it is constant or intermittent, and anything already tried.
  8. Submit.
Please set the business impact honestly

These targets are real commitments we measure ourselves against. Marking a minor inconvenience as Critical pulls engineers away from genuinely failed systems. If something is genuinely down, do mark it Critical — that is what the level is for.

Working the ticket with us

  1. We triage the ticket and assign an engineer.
  2. The engineer posts a first reply in the conversation. Reply in the same thread — it keeps everything in one place.
  3. If we need something from you, the ticket moves to Awaiting your information and is flagged on your Dashboard. Replying moves it straight back to us, so your answer is all that is needed.
  4. When the engineer believes it is fixed, the ticket moves to Resolved, with What we did and the Root cause recorded.
  5. You then decide. If it is genuinely fixed, close the ticket. If it is not, reopen it and say why — it goes straight back to the engineer.
Why you close the ticket, not us

Only you can confirm a problem has actually gone away at your customer's site. An engineer cannot close a ticket, precisely so that "closed" means the customer agreed — not that we decided we were finished. Closing promptly when something is fixed genuinely helps; so does reopening when it is not.

6.10 Managing your company's portal users

Partner Admin only.

  1. Open your company's portal users area.
  2. To add a colleague, create an account with their name and e-mail address. They receive a welcome message with a one-time password and must set their own password on first use, exactly as you did.
  3. To remove access — when a colleague leaves, or changes role — deactivate their account. Do this promptly.
  4. Review your company's portal users periodically and remove anyone who no longer needs access.
This is your responsibility, not ours

We cannot know when someone at your company leaves. Keeping your portal user list current is the single most effective thing you can do to protect your own customer information.

If you cannot find this area

Only the Partner Admin account can manage users. If you believe you hold that role and cannot see the option, contact your relationship manager. The exact location of this area in the menu is To be confirmed.

12.2 Recommended practice

Getting good answers quickly

  • Submit opportunities early, even when details are incomplete — an early submission with remarks beats a perfect one three weeks later.
  • Name the decision-maker and the budget position in Remarks. These are the two things we most often have to ask about.
  • Tick every department the deal might involve. Too few means the right team never sees it.
  • Reply promptly when something is flagged as waiting on you. Nothing moves until you do.

Accurate information

  • Give a realistic approximate value. It is an estimate, not a commitment, and an honest one helps us prioritise properly.
  • Use the business-impact levels honestly on support tickets.
  • Always include the model or serial number when you have it. It is the single most useful field on a support ticket.
  • Agree a date with your customer before requesting a demonstration, and offer an alternative.

Keeping everything in one place

  • Use the conversation threads rather than e-mail or phone for anything that matters. A conversation attached to the record is visible to whoever picks it up next.
  • Attach documents at submission rather than sending them separately.
  • Close resolved tickets when the fix works, and reopen them when it does not. Both are genuinely useful to us.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Check your existing submissions before adding a new one for the same customer.
  • Search Marketing Materials before raising a marketing request — it may already exist.
  • Reopen an existing ticket for a recurrence rather than raising a second one.

Account security — your responsibilities

  • One account per person. Never share a login, even within your own team.
  • Use a unique password, stored in a password manager rather than written down or reused.
  • Sign out on shared or public computers.
  • Remove access promptly when a colleague leaves. We cannot know when that happens.
  • Review your portal users periodically — quarterly is a sensible rhythm.
  • Report anything suspicious immediately, including a compromised account or data you did not expect to see.

Handling what you download

  • Brochures, datasheets, case studies and presentations are published for you to use with your customers.
  • Quotations are specific to one customer and one opportunity. Do not reuse one for another customer.
  • Treat everything else as confidential to your business relationship with us.

14Glossary and Appendix

How badly a fault affects the customer. It sets our response and resolution targets, so choose it honestly.
Collateral
Marketing material — brochures, datasheets, case studies, presentations, banners and similar.
Conversation thread
The record of messages attached to a submission, a request or a ticket. Both sides read the same thread.
Dashboard
Your home screen, showing activity, value and anything waiting on you.
Demonstration mode
Whether a demo is given at the customer's site, with equipment shipped, or online with nothing shipped.
Information requested
The position a submission takes when we have asked you a question. The one circumstance in which you may edit it.
Opportunity
A potential piece of business you submit to us. Once accepted it becomes a project in our pipeline.
Partner Admin
The primary portal account for your company. Also manages your company's other portal users.
Partner User
A portal account for a team member at your company.
Pipeline
The sequence an accepted opportunity moves through: Prospect, Demo / Qualified, Quotation, Negotiation, Commit, then Won or Lost.
Relationship manager
The person at our company assigned to your partner account. Your first point of contact.
Resolution target
The time within which we aim to resolve a ticket of a given business impact. Time spent waiting on your answer is not counted.
Response target
The time within which an engineer will post a first substantive reply.
Validity
The period for which a quotation's pricing holds good.
Version (quotation)
A revision of a quotation. Earlier versions remain visible so the history is intact.
