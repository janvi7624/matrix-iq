02Document Control

2.1 Purpose of this document

This SOP explains how HR and Administration staff use MatrixIQ to maintain the employee directory, run the reimbursement cycle, record office and company-paid spend, report on all of it, manage business travel, and review the company's daily reporting regime.

It is also written for the employees and managers who feed these processes — because almost every HR workflow in MatrixIQ begins with someone outside HR. Section 6 therefore covers both sides of the reimbursement and travel chains.

2.5 Scope and limitations

In scope. The seven HR submodules, the reimbursement and travel approval chains end to end, and the HR half of the daily reporting regime.

Out of scope. Payroll, statutory compliance, recruitment, performance appraisal mechanics, and the settlement of payments — which belongs to Accounts.

  • Three of these modules are granted to named individuals rather than to the HR role. Holding the HR role does not open them. See §4.2.
  • Approval routing depends on Department Master. A change there alters who approves what, with no notice.
  • Details the application could not confirm are marked To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.
Confidentiality

These screens expose identifiable employees' expense claims, travel, contact details and reporting compliance. Access is deliberately narrow. Do not widen it informally, do not export to shared locations, and do not discuss an individual's claims or warnings outside the people who must act on them.

03Introduction

3.1 What the HR module does

The HR module is where people and money meet: who works here, what they spent, where they travelled, and whether they reported their day.

It covers three distinct kinds of work. First, the directory — who is employed, in which department, with what contact details. Second, spend — three separate registers for money claimed back by employees, money the company paid on their behalf, and money spent running the offices, together with one report that reads all three. Third, process stewardship — holding the HR decision in the reimbursement and travel chains, and reviewing leave claims and missed-report warnings arising from the daily reporting regime.

3.2 Why it exists

  • A claim should not depend on a spreadsheet. Reimbursement runs as a monthly sheet with a real approval chain, bill attachments and an Excel voucher at the end.
  • Approval should follow the organisation, not a hard-coded name. Every HR approval resolves from Department Master, so a change of manager changes the routing automatically.
  • Spend should be answerable in one place. The HR Expense Report reads reimbursement, admin expense and office operation spend together, by period, employee or department.
  • Reporting compliance should be visible but humane. A day covered by an approved leave claim is never counted as missing, and a warning can be waived with a reason.

04Access and Permissions

4.1 Two different gates

The HR submodules are not all protected the same way. There are two patterns, and confusing them is the most common source of "why can't I open this?".

4.2 The named-individual gate, explained

Holding the HR role does not open the three spend registers

Office Operation Expenses, Admin Expenses and the HR Expense Report are granted to particular people by username, on top of Super Admin and Admin. This was a deliberate narrowing: one person needs the office register, a different person needs admin expenses, and other HR accounts need neither.

This grant cannot be changed from Role Management or Module Manager. Requests to add or remove a person go to the platform's technical owner. Escalation route: to be confirmed.

4.4 Reading is not acting — the daily report split

The clearest example of a deliberate permission split in MatrixIQ

HR reads every department's daily reports. HR can edit and file reports only within its own department. Widening HR's visibility was deliberate; widening its authority was not. The same split applies to department managers elsewhere.

Specifically, for an employee outside HR's own department an HR user can view the report but cannot edit it and cannot file one on their behalf. Only Admin and Super Admin, who see organisation-wide, hold both.

5.1 Two things the reference describes that are not where you would expect

Reference discrepancies — verified against the application

Attendance view. The Feature Reference lists an attendance view under Employees. The attendance module has been retired from MatrixIQ: its registration is removed, and opening its address returns the user to the dashboard. There is no reachable attendance screen. Whether attendance is tracked elsewhere is To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application.

Birthdays and work anniversaries. These are not on an HR module page. They appear in a Celebrations panel on the main MatrixIQ dashboard, visible to everyone who signs in, showing upcoming birthdays and work anniversaries with a Today, Tomorrow or in N days label.

Employee exit. The exit checklist, summary and commit are not part of Employees. They are a separate module at /employee-exit in the Administration section, open to anyone who manages a department. See the Administration Module SOP §6.1.5.

06Detailed Step-by-Step Procedures

6.1 Employees

Preconditions

  • You hold the HR, Admin or Super Admin role. To create an account you must additionally be an HR department manager, or Admin / Super Admin.

Procedure — browsing the directory

  1. Open HR → Employees. The active employee directory is listed.
  2. Use the filter bar to narrow by department, or search for a person.
  3. Open a row to read the full profile — name, username, employee ID, designation, department, role, e-mail and phone.

Procedure — adding an employee

  1. Select the add control. It is present only for HR managers, Admin and Super Admin.
  2. Complete: name, username, password, phone, e-mail, employee ID, designation, department and role. There is also a tick to mark the person a department manager.
  3. Save. A welcome e-mail is sent if the platform's e-mail service is configured.
Marking someone a department manager has wide consequences

Department managers approve reimbursements and travel for their team, gain sight of their team's daily reports and tasks, and in some departments gain access to whole modules — the Accounts payment queue and Back Office operations among them. Do not set this tick as a courtesy title. See the Administration Module SOP §6.3.

Procedure — editing an employee

  1. Select Edit on the person's row.
  2. Change name, phone, e-mail, employee ID, designation or role as needed, then save.
  3. For deactivation, password resets and bulk import, use Administration → User Management.
Super Admin accounts are not listed

Super Admin accounts are invisible to every other role, including Admin, by design. Their absence from the directory is not a fault.

6.2 Reimbursement — the employee's side

Every employee uses this. The screen opens on the My Sheet tab. Reviewers additionally see Pending Approvals and Approved.

  1. Open Reimbursement. Set the month and year in the toolbar.
  2. Select + Add Entry. If the button is absent, read the hint beside it — new entries can only be added for the current or previous month.
  3. Date * — the date of the expense.
  4. Description * — choose one of: Lunch, Dinner, Snacks, Conveyance, Bus Ticket, Train Ticket, Flight Ticket, Hotel, Parking Fees, Other. Choosing Other opens a free-text box, which is then required.
  5. If you chose Conveyance, a Vehicle Type * choice appears:

    4 Wheeler₹8 per km

  7. For Conveyance, Bus Ticket, Train Ticket and Flight Ticket, enter From and To. Both are required for Conveyance.
  8. For Conveyance by 2 Wheeler or 4 Wheeler, enter Kilometers *. The amount is calculated.
  9. Amount (₹) * — enter it unless it has been calculated. The amount in words is shown back to you.
  10. Mode of Payment — Cash, UPI, Bank Transfer, Credit Card, Debit Card, Cheque or Other.
  11. Attach the bill proof — images or PDF, up to 10 MB per file. More than one may be attached.

6.2.3 Replacing a bill without losing the entry

If a bill image is wrong or unreadable, edit the entry and attach the correct file. The claim's date, description, amount and approval position are preserved — you do not have to delete and re-create it.

6.2.4 The missing-bills panel

What "tracking missing bills" actually means

A panel appears at the top of the Reimbursement screen only when a bill file can no longer be opened. This is a recovery tool, not a general tracker of claims submitted without a bill: an earlier file store was lost, so some claims reference bill images that no longer exist. The claims themselves — date, description, amount, who — are intact.

If the panel appears for you, it lists your affected claims with one upload control per lost file. Send the original again — a photo or the e-mailed receipt. This works even on claims that have already been approved, which the ordinary edit form will not touch. HR, Accounts and Admin additionally see who is affected and a control that asks everyone concerned to re-upload, by notification and e-mail. The panel disappears entirely once every bill can be opened again.

6.2.5 Submitting the sheet

  1. Check the Monthly Total and that every claim has its bill.
  2. Read the deadline line at the top of the screen. It states the submission deadline day for that month, and whether it has been extended and by whom.
  3. Select Submit to Manager for Approval. The button is disabled if the month is not yet submittable.
  4. The sheet's status becomes Awaiting Manager Approval and the claims are locked.
If you manage a department

A sheet created by a department manager skips manager review entirely and goes straight to HR. Its status reads Sent to HR rather than "Manager Approved", because no human manager actually reviewed it. This is correct behaviour.

6.2.6 Responding to a change request

  1. When a reviewer asks for changes, the sheet's status becomes Changes Requested (Manager) or Changes Requested (HR), and the reviewer's remarks are shown.
  2. The sheet is editable again. New entries may be added whatever month it is — the usual add window does not apply to a sheet sent back for correction.
  3. Make the requested corrections and submit again.

6.2.7 Exporting the voucher

With entries present, select Export Voucher to download the month's expense voucher as an Excel file. From a sheet detail, Download Voucher does the same. Reviewers can also merge every attached bill for a sheet into a single PDF for checking.

6.3 Reimbursement — manager and HR review

6.3.1 Manager decision

  1. Open Reimbursement and select the Pending Approvals tab. It carries a count.
  2. Open a sheet. Read the entries, the Monthly Total, and open the bill proofs.
  3. Under Your Decision, approve or request changes. Remarks are optional for an approval and required when requesting changes.
  4. On approval the sheet moves to HR. On a change request it returns to the employee with your remarks.

6.3.2 HR decision

  1. Open the Pending Approvals tab. Sheets at Manager Approved are awaiting you.
  2. Open the sheet and check it against policy — eligibility, rates, duplicates, whether a bill is genuinely attached where one is required.
  3. Under HR Review, approve or request changes. Remarks are required for a change request.
  4. On approval the status becomes HR Approved and the sheet enters the Accounts payment queue. HR's involvement ends there.

6.3.3 Extending a month's deadline

  1. On the Reimbursement screen, find the deadline line at the top.
  2. Select Extend this month's deadline — visible only to those authorised.
  3. Enter the new day of the month (1–31) and Save.
  4. The line now shows the new day and records that it was extended, and by whom. Extensions are additive history; the most recent one governs.

The default deadline day for every month is set in Administration → Application Settings → Reimbursement Settings, and may be left blank for no deadline.

6.3.4 Deleting an approved but unpaid claim

Permanent, and deliberately difficult

An approved-but-unpaid sheet can be removed. MatrixIQ requires a written reason — "Why is this being deleted?" — and requires you to type a confirmation. This is a real deletion: the sheet and the employee's own bill entries for that month are gone and cannot be recovered.

Two things are deliberately kept: Admin Expense entries, which belong to a different module and may cover other employees; and the uploaded bill images themselves, because orphaning a file is recoverable and deleting the wrong one is not.

Use this only for a genuine duplicate or an erroneous claim, with the reason recorded. It is not a correction tool — for that, request changes instead.

6.4 Office Operation Expenses

The HR/Admin office operating spend register, covering office, electricity, guest, director, salary and pantry expenses among others. Named-individual access — see §4.2.

6.4.1 Recording an expense

  1. Open Office Operation Expenses. Set the month and year.
  2. Select + Add Expense. The form notes that the Sr No. is assigned automatically on save.
  3. Complete the fields:
    FieldRequiredNotes
    Date *RequiredThe date of the expense.
    Office Location *RequiredAhmedabad or Mumbai.
    Category *RequiredOffice, Employee, Guest, Director or Salary — as configured. A sub-level appears only for categories that have one.
    Expense Head *RequiredStationary and Printing, Pantry, Water Jug, Porter, Courier and Postage, Tempo, Miscellaneous, Electricity, Internet, CUG, IT Materials, Salary, Legal, HR, Department, Godown 30, Godown 31, Flowers, Maintenance, Rent, Training, EXPO / Event — as configured.     Item QtyOptionalLeave blank where a count is meaningless, such as an electricity bill. A supplied value must be greater than zero. Blank is deliberately different from a quantity of 1.
    Amount (₹) *RequiredMust be greater than zero.
    DescriptionOptionalWhat was purchased or paid for.
    Paid By *RequiredOne of the named payers, or Other — which opens a required name box.
  4. Save. If the entry's date falls outside the month on screen, the view jumps to that month so the saved row does not silently vanish.
Reference discrepancy — there is no bill attachment

The Feature Reference lists "attach the bill" for this module. The Office Operation Expenses form has no attachment field and the record carries no bill document. (The only documents the record can hold are the payment-proof files Accounts adds when settling.) Keep bills by whatever means the organisation requires outside MatrixIQ, and use Description to record a bill or invoice number. Document-retention requirement: to be confirmed.

6.4.2 Editing the Category and Expense Head lists

  1. Select Categories in the toolbar. The option editor opens.
  2. Add, rename or remove Category and Expense Head values, and their sub-levels.
  3. Save. The form, the validation behind it and the Excel export all use the edited lists immediately.
How edits sit on the built-in lists

MatrixIQ ships a default vocabulary. A list you have never edited follows that default and gains any future additions to it. Once you edit a list, your version governs — including deliberately emptying one, which is remembered as a decision rather than treated as "never configured". Removing a parent value also removes its sub-list, since that sub-list could never be reached again.

Renaming a value does not re-label past entries

Entries already recorded keep the text that was stored at the time. Renaming a category will split a period report between the old and new spelling. Prefer adding a new value and retiring the old one at a period boundary.

6.4.3 Reading, editing and exporting the register

  1. The table shows Sr No., Date, Office, Category, Expense Head, Qty, Amount, Description, Paid By, Entered By and Actions, with a month total.
  2. Use the row actions to edit an entry. The serial number never changes once assigned.
  3. Select Export .xlsx to download the month's register, prepared in your name. The control is disabled when the month has no entries.
Deletion here is permanent

Unlike most of MatrixIQ, the office expense register does not keep a recoverable copy of a deleted row. A deletion cannot be undone. Edit rather than delete wherever possible.

6.4.4 How the register reaches Accounts

Every unpaid entry for a calendar month forms one payable sheet in the Accounts payment queue. There is no approval step on the entry itself — an entry becomes payable simply by existing. The control over this spend is the restricted access to the register, not an approval chain. Record entries accurately and promptly, because an entry added late in the month changes a sheet Accounts may already be looking at.

6.5 Admin Expenses

Costs the company pays directly on employees' behalf, often shared across several people. Named-individual access — see §4.2.

6.5.1 Recording an expense

  1. Open Admin Expenses and start a new entry.
  2. Expense Type * — Hotel, Visa Expense, Bus Ticket, Train Ticket, Flight Ticket or Other Expense.
  3. Complete the fields the type calls for:
    For a…Fields
    HotelCheck-in Date *, Check-out Date *, Location *
    Ticket (bus, train, flight)From *, To *, Trip Type * (One-Way or Return), and Return Date * for a return trip
    Visa ExpenseDetails (optional) — for example US Visa, UK Visa Renewal
    Other ExpenseSpecify Type * — for example Taxi, Courier, Parking — and Location / Details (optional)
  4. Total Amount (₹) * — the whole cost, not the per-person share.
  5. Choose the employees the expense covers. Search the list, or use Select All and Clear. A Split Preview shows the resulting per-person figure.
  6. Save.
How the split is accounted

Each employee receives their own row carrying their share. Reports and the Accounts queue sum the per-person figures, never the batch total repeated on each row — so a multi-employee expense is counted once.

6.5.2 Approval

  1. A batch created by anyone other than the single named approver is created as Pending Approval.
  2. The named approver opens the batch, checks it, and approves. A batch created by the named approver themselves needs no separate sign-off.
  3. On approval, Accounts is notified. Approved entries for a calendar month then form one payable sheet.
Unapproved entries never reach Accounts

Only approved admin expense entries are swept into a month's payable sheet, even if their date falls within it. An entry left pending sign-off will quietly never be paid. Clear the pending list before each month end.

The register lists Date, Type, Location / Route, Total (₹), Employees, Per Person (₹), Added, Status and Actions.

6.6 HR Expense Report

One report across all three spend sources. Named-individual access — see §4.2.

  1. Open HR → HR Expense Report.
  2. Set the period — a from and to date — and the granularity: Monthly, Quarterly or Yearly. Selectors for All Months and All Quarters narrow further.
  3. Choose the expense types to include: Reimbursement, Admin Expense, Office Operation. Omitting the choice includes all three; at least one must be selected.
  4. Narrow by All Departments or All Employees as required.
  5. Tick approved entries only to exclude anything not yet signed off.
  6. Choose how to group the result — by employee, by department, or by source.
  7. Read the summary tiles, then the tables: By Expense Type and Overall, each showing Entries and Total.
  8. Select the export control to download the result. Reset clears the filters.
The export always matches the screen

The report and its export read the same filters from the same place, so an export can never disagree with the figures you were looking at. Note the filters alongside any file you circulate.

Dates are read as local calendar dates

Expense dates are treated as plain calendar dates, so an expense on the first of a month is never filed under the previous one. Figures are therefore stable regardless of who runs the report.

6.7 Travel Schedule

6.7.1 Raising a travel request

  1. Open Travel Schedule and select New Travel Request.
  2. Origin * and Destination *.
  3. Under When do you need to reach the destination? — Arrival Date * and Arrival Time.
  4. Under When do you want to leave the destination? — Departure Date and Departure Time.
  5. Project(s) — one trip can cover several projects at the destination. Add each one.
  6. Linked Client and Travel Companions — companions are chosen from MatrixIQ users.
  7. Mode of Travel — Plane, Train or Cab.
  8. Purpose of Travel — Meeting, Demo, Site Survey, Event or Others. Choosing Others opens Specify Purpose *.
  9. Suggest a Flight / Train / Cab (optional) — free text, for example IndiGo 6E-234, morning departure. Accounts considers it when booking; it is never binding.
  10. Add Co-Traveller — for people who are not MatrixIQ users. Each needs a Name *, and optionally contact, origin, destination and travel date.
  11. Hotel Accommodation — if required: Preferred Area *, Suggested Hotel, Location, Check-in Date *, Check-out Date *, Guests, Additional Requirement.
  12. Advance Request — if required: Requested Amount (₹) * and a Reason / Remark.
  13. Save. The request is created as a Draft.
  14. Open it and select Submit for Approval.

6.7.2 Manager decision

  1. Open the request from the Travel Schedule list.
  2. Read the route, dates, purpose, projects, companions and any hotel or advance request.
  3. Select Manager Approve or Request Changes, with remarks.

Only a manager of the traveller's own department may act, or an Admin / Super Admin. Anyone else is refused: "Only the department manager can approve this request". The stage is available only while the request is at Submitted.

6.7.3 HR review

  1. Open a request at Manager Approved.
  2. Check it against travel policy — necessity, duration, accommodation and any advance.
  3. Record an estimated cost and attach any HR documents.
  4. Select HR Approve or Request Changes.

Only a manager of the HR department may act, or an Admin / Super Admin: "Only HR can review this request".

6.7.4 Administration decision

  1. Open a request at HR Reviewed.
  2. Select Admin Approve or Request Changes, with remarks.

Only a manager of the Admin or Administration department may act, or an Admin / Super Admin: "Only Admin can approve this request".

6.7.5 Ticket booking — the Accounts stage

This stage is not HR's. Once the request is at Admin Approved, the Accounts team selects Complete Booking, enters the Booking / Ticket Details * (ticket numbers, PNR, flight or train details, timings), attaches the Ticket Documents, and records the actual cost. MatrixIQ refuses anyone else: "Only the Accounts team can complete ticket booking". On completion the status becomes Ticket Booked and HR is notified that final verification is needed.

6.7.6 HR final verification

  1. Open a request at Ticket Booked.
  2. Check the booking details and ticket documents against what was approved, and compare the Actual Cost with the Estimated Cost.
  3. Select Verify & Confirm to complete the request, or Send Back.
  4. If sending back, choose a destination:
    Send back toUse when
    Employee (for corrections)The traveller must change something about the request.
    Department Manager (re-verify)The trip has changed enough that the manager should look again.
    HR Department (re-verify)The HR review itself needs revisiting.
    Provide remarks explaining what is wrong.
  5. On confirmation the status becomes Completed and the traveller is notified.

6.8 Daily Report Review

Open HR → Daily Report Review. The screen has two tabs: Leave Claims and Warnings. You must be an HR department manager (or Admin / Super Admin) to decide anything here.

6.8.1 Deciding a leave claim

  1. Open the Leave Claims tab. Filter by Pending, Approved, Rejected or All, and search by employee. Pending claims are listed first.
  2. Read the row: Employee, Department, Reporting date, Reason, Submitted at, Status, Reviewed by, Decision reason.
  3. Select Approve or Reject. A Reason * is required.
DecisionEffect on the reporting obligation
ApproveThe date is excused. A day covered by an approved leave claim is never counted as missing.
RejectGoverned by the configured policy: either reinstate the reporting obligation — the day counts as missed again, which is the configured default — or waive the obligation for that date. The policy is set in Daily Report Configuration.
A pending claim already excuses the day

A claim that is still awaiting your decision counts as live, so the date is not treated as missed while you consider it. An employee cannot raise a second claim for a date that already has a live claim.

6.8.2 Reviewing and waiving warnings

  1. Open the Warnings tab. Filter by With active warnings, At or over the threshold, or All, including fully waived, and search by employee.
  2. Warnings are grouped by employee and month. A group showing HR informed has reached the configured threshold — three active warnings in a month, as configured.
  3. To excuse one, select Waive and enter a Reason *, for example "Employee was on approved leave; claim raised late."
  4. The warning becomes Waived. The record is not deleted — it leaves the active count while remaining visible in the history, which is what keeps the trail honest.
Reinstating a waived warning

MatrixIQ's warning interface accepts a reinstate action as well as a waive, but no reinstate control was found on the Warnings screen. Whether reinstating is available to HR in practice is To be verified in the live MatrixIQ application. Treat a waiver as final and record the reason carefully.

6.8.3 What this screen is not

Reviewing is not editing or filing

Daily Report Review decides leave claims and warnings. It is not where reports are read in bulk, edited, or filed on someone's behalf — that is Team Daily Reports, and there HR's rights are narrower: HR reads every department but may edit and file only within its own. Only Admin and Super Admin hold both company-wide.

12.2 Recommended practice

Accurate data entry

  • Enter claims as they happen, not in a rush at month end. The add window gives you two months, no more.
  • Choose the most specific description. Other makes a claim harder to review and harder to report on.
  • Record the real kilometres. The amount follows from them, and a round number invites scrutiny.
  • In the office register, use Description to record the bill or invoice number — there is nowhere else to put it.

Documents

  • Attach a legible bill. If a photograph is poor, replace it rather than leaving it.
  • Attach HR documents at the travel review stage where the trip needs supporting paperwork.
  • Keep office expense bills outside MatrixIQ, filed so that a register row can be matched to one. Retention requirement: to be confirmed.

Timely approvals

  • Clear Pending Approvals at a fixed point each week. A sheet held at a manager delays the employee's money, not the manager's.
  • Decide leave claims promptly — a pending claim is excusing a date that may not deserve it.
  • Clear pending admin expense approvals before each month end, or they miss the month's sheet.
  • Verify travel bookings as soon as Accounts completes them, while the trip can still be corrected.

Confidentiality and access control

  • Treat every screen here as confidential. Do not leave a reimbursement list or a warnings tab visible on a shared screen.
  • Never share an account. Every decision is attributed to the signed-in user.
  • Discuss an individual's claims, travel or warnings only with the people who must act on them.
  • Keep the named-individual grants narrow. If someone no longer needs the office or admin expense registers, have the grant removed.

Avoiding duplicates

  • Check the month's existing entries before adding a claim, especially after a change request.
  • A shared meal should be one entry naming everyone present, not one entry per person.
  • In the office register, search the month before recording a recurring bill such as rent or electricity.

Keeping an auditable history

  • Use change requests rather than deletion. A change request leaves a trail; deletion destroys one.
  • Write decision remarks that will still make sense in six months.
  • Waive warnings with a specific reason, never a generic one.
  • Record who authorised an extension to a month's reimbursement deadline.

14Glossary and Appendix

14.1 Glossary

Add window
The rule limiting brand-new reimbursement claims to the current or previous month. Lifted on a sheet sent back for changes.
Admin Expense
A cost the company pays directly on employees' behalf — hotel, tickets, visa — often split across several people.
Batch
One admin expense entry covering several employees, created and approved as a unit. It is no longer the unit of payment, which is the calendar month.
Celebrations
The dashboard panel showing upcoming birthdays and work anniversaries.
Change request
A reviewer returning a sheet or request to its owner with remarks, rather than refusing it outright.
Conveyance
Travel by the employee's own vehicle, claimed per kilometre: ₹4 for a 2 Wheeler, ₹8 for a 4 Wheeler. A Cab is claimed at actual cost with a bill.
Department manager
Someone listed as a manager of a department in Department Master. This relationship, not a role, drives approval routing.
Expense Head
The second classification level on an office operation expense — what the money was for. Editable in the application.
HR manager
A manager of the department whose name begins with "HR". Holds the HR decision in reimbursement, travel and daily report review.
Leave claim
An employee's request that a particular date be excused from daily reporting.
Named-individual grant
Access granted to particular usernames rather than to a role. Used for the three HR and Administration spend modules.
Paid By
On an office operation expense, who fronted the money. A short list of named payers plus a free-text option.
Sheet (reimbursement)
One employee's claims for one calendar month, approved and paid as a single unit.
Sheet (monthly expense)
For office and admin expenses, all qualifying entries for one calendar month grouped into a single payable item for Accounts.
Sweep
The scheduled job that checks who has filed a daily report, sends reminders and raises warnings. Safe to repeat.
Voucher
The Excel document produced from a month's reimbursement sheet.
Warning (missed report)
A record that an employee did not file on a working day. Can be waived with a reason; the record is kept.
